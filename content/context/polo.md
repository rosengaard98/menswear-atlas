British tea planters and army officers met polo in Manipur, in north-east India, in the 1850s. By 1859 they had founded a club in Assam, and ten years later the first match on English soil was played on Hounslow Heath in 1869. In 1874 the Hurlingham Polo Committee drew up the first set of rules.

## What the players wore

Until the early twentieth century polo players wore thick, long-sleeved shirts of [[oxford-cloth|oxford cotton]]. At speed the collar points flew up, so players buttoned them to the shirt. That small fix is the origin of the [[button-down-collar]]: according to the firm, John Brooks saw it at a match in England and brought it home to [[brooks-brothers]] in New York, where it became the [[ocbd]].

## Two kinds of polo shirt

The game lent its name to two different shirts. Brooks called its button-down a polo shirt. Today the phrase means something else: the short-sleeved knit shirt that [[rene-lacoste|René Lacoste]] wore on the tennis court in 1926. Earlier still, in 1920, the Canadian-born polo player and haberdasher Lewis Lacey began making a shirt embroidered with a polo player, a design from the Hurlingham club near Buenos Aires. In 1972 [[ralph-lauren|Ralph Lauren]] sold a knit tennis shirt as a polo shirt in his Polo line, which did most to fix the name. See [[polo-shirt]].
