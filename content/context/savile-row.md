Savile Row was a street of houses before it was a street of tailors. It was built between 1731 and 1735 on the Burlington Estate, on land called Ten Acres that belonged to the merchant tailor William Maddox. It was first called Savile Street, after Lady Dorothy Savile, wife of the 3rd Earl of Burlington. Its first residents were army officers and politicians.

## The tailors arrive

Tailors had reached nearby Cork Street by about 1790. That is where [[beau-brummell|Beau Brummell]]'s tailors worked, rather than on the Row. Who came first to Savile Row depends on the source: Wikipedia's Savile Row page says tailors were there by 1803 while its page on Savile Row tailoring says 1806, *The Rake* says 1805, and the London Museum names [[henry-poole|Henry Poole]], which opened a Savile Row entrance to its Old Burlington Street premises in 1846, as probably the first on the Row itself. Poole's house tradition credits it with the dinner jacket of [[evening-dress|evening wear]], made as a smoking jacket for the future Edward VII; Wikipedia dates it to 1860.

[[huntsman|H. Huntsman & Sons]] moved onto the Row in 1919, [[anderson-sheppard|Anderson & Sheppard]] was founded in 1906, and Hawkes & Co took No. 1 in 1912, later becoming [[gieves-and-hawkes|Gieves & Hawkes]], credited as the first Row house to sell ready-to-wear. In the early 1900s [[frederick-scholte|Frederick Scholte]]'s English [[drape-cut|drape]] was popularised with Anderson & Sheppard, where his protégé Per Anderson made the London cut the house style.

## New names

Hardy Amies opened at No. 14 in 1946 according to Wikipedia, or 1949 according to the London Museum. The Beatles' Apple Corps took No. 3 in 1968. On 14 February 1969 (Wikipedia's date) [[tommy-nutter|Tommy Nutter]] and Edward Sexton opened Nutters of Savile Row at No. 35a, backed by Cilla Black and Peter Brown. *The Rake* calls it the first new firm on the Row in generations. Three of the four Beatles wore Nutters on the *Abbey Road* cover. In the 1990s Ozwald Boateng, Timothy Everest and Richard James brought brighter colour and celebrity clients, in what was called the New Bespoke Movement.

## Bespoke today

The London Museum defines bespoke as made to one customer's measurements over several fittings. In 2008 the Advertising Standards Authority allowed the word for machine-sewn made-to-measure. Since 2004 the Savile Row Bespoke Association has required at least 50 hours of hand work in a member's two-piece suit. In 2016 Kathryn Sargent became the first woman to open a house on the Row. Many houses now sell ready-to-wear and made-to-measure too. *The Rake*'s writer also asserts that labels such as [[ralph-lauren|Ralph Lauren]], Tom Ford and Hackett looked to the Row for inspiration.
