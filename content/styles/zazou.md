While cloth was rationed in occupied Paris, the zazous wore jackets that reached the knee. Wikipedia reads the extra cloth as mockery of government decrees, though no statement from the zazous themselves says so. They were a youth set of [[vichy-france|Vichy France]], active from about 1940 to 1944, mostly in Paris. Wikipedia puts most of them between 17 and 20 and numbers them in the hundreds; Timeline describes them as typically educated and well-off.

Swing was the point. Interwar Montmartre had a Black American jazz scene, and [[django-reinhardt|Django Reinhardt]] played [[swing-jazz|swing]] in Paris clubs. [[johnny-hess|Johnny Hess]]'s "Je suis swing" was a best-selling record, though Wikipedia dates it to early 1942 and Semiovox to 1938. The name probably comes from a [[cab-calloway|Cab Calloway]] song, whose title the sources give differently, and [[boris-vian|Boris Vian]] is linked to the scene. Older, more middle-class zazous sat on the terrace of the Pam Pam on the Champs-Élysées; others met in Latin Quarter cellar clubs such as the Dupont-Latin and the Capoulade.

## The clothes

Wikipedia and *Organise!* describe the men's look: long jackets with many pockets and half-belts, narrow trousers gathered at the waist, narrow ties in cotton or heavy wool, high shirt collars held by a horizontal pin, thick-soled suede shoes with white or bright socks, and long greased hair. Timeline adds tiny moustaches. W. D. Halls, quoted by Wikipedia, mentions [[drape-cut|drape]] suits, drainpipe trousers and sheepskin-lined jackets. Checks turned up on jackets, skirts and [[umbrella|umbrellas]]. The umbrella stayed furled, even in sunshine; Timeline and *Organise!* say it was called a "Chamberlain" and parodied English style, while Wikipedia simply reads it as British.

Women wore wide-shouldered jackets, short pleated skirts, striped or net stockings, heavy wooden-soled shoes, red lipstick and sunglasses.

Wikipedia says the look echoed the American [[zoot-suit|zoot suit]] and that the long jacket followed suits worn by performers like Calloway. Semiovox calls it only zoot-suit-like. No source shows the zazous copying the zoot suit directly.

## The reaction

Timeline names *La Gerbe*, *Gringoire* and *Le Cri du Peuple* among papers that attacked them. Wikipedia says the Jeunesse Populaire Française made them "Enemy Number One" and sent squads with clippers to shave their heads. After the June 1942 yellow star edict, some non-Jewish zazous wore stars lettered "zazou", "swing" or "goy". From July 1942 came café raids and labour in the countryside, and the scene faded with forced labour in 1942-43.

Whether this was resistance is disputed. David Drake, quoted by Semiovox, calls them rebels rather than resisters, and *Organise!* says the Communist resistance thought them apathetic. Kelly Jakes, in a 2013 seminar abstract, argues their swing identity stood against Vichy racial ideology.
