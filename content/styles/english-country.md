English country dress began as sporting kit for the upper classes. [[tweed|Tweed]] was first their cloth for shooting jackets; in Edwardian years the middle classes took it up too, in Norfolk jackets and plus-fours for hunting, cycling, golf and early motoring.

## Tweed and its checks

The cloth was first called tweel, Scots for twill. The usual story is that a clerk misread the word as tweed: Britannica places this in London in 1826, Wikipedia around 1831 in a letter to the merchant James Locke. Britannica says the name does not come from the River Tweed.

[[estate-tweed|Estate tweeds]], or district checks, were woven to mark the people of one estate. Victorian visitors who rented Scottish estates had their staff dressed in them, with no tie to any local tartan, and Johnstons wove many. Which came first is disputed. Gentleman's Gazette names Glenfeshie, around 1835, made for ghillies and keepers; Permanent Style credits Janie Ellice, a tenant at Glen Feshie, who put a scarlet overcheck on shepherd's check. They may be the same check, but neither source says so.

Some checks travelled. The [[glen-check|Glen Urquhart]], woven locally by Elizabeth MacDougall and adopted by the Countess of Seafield for the Balmacaan estate, was recoloured in brown and cream at a smaller scale by the future [[edward-vii|Edward VII]]. [[duke-of-windsor|Edward VIII]] added red and blue overchecks, and the result is now called Prince of Wales check.

[[prince-albert|Prince Albert]] is credited with making Scotland a shooting destination. [[harris-tweed|Harris Tweed]] reached the aristocracy in the 1840s through Lady Catherine Murray, Countess of Dunmore; its Orb mark dates from 1909.

## The kit

The [[norfolk-jacket]] is a loose, belted, single-breasted tweed shooting coat with box pleats front and back and patch pockets, cut so the wearer can raise his elbow to fire. It was fashionable after the 1860s. Its namesake is unsettled: Coke of Norfolk, the 15th Duke of Norfolk and the county are all offered, and Wikipedia finds none well supported. Edward VII is said to have made it popular at Sandringham, though Wikipedia knows no picture of him in one. The retailer Westwood Hart says the belt shrank to a half belt and then went, leaving the modern sports jacket by the 1930s.

[[brogue|Brogues]] descend from rough hide shoes of Ireland and Scotland, and their name is Gaelic for shoe. They were country footwear, long thought unsuitable for business. [[barbour|Barbour]] was founded in South Shields in 1894 as an oilcloth importer and made weatherproofs for the forces in the Second World War. Wikipedia, citing *The Gentleman's Journal*, dates its waxed Beaufort hunting jacket to 1983.
