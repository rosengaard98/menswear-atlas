Until the 20th century, the Austrian encyclopedia AEIOU says, Tracht meant the dress that marked a person's legal status, religion, rank or trade. The idea of a timeless rustic costume came later, AEIOU says most local forms are fairly recent and that townspeople and aristocrats sparked the interest in keeping them; the *Jüdische Allgemeine* goes further and calls them newly created.

## Courts and clubs

Wikipedia ties the revival to the Bavarian and Austrian courts after the Napoleonic wars. [[archduke-johann|Archduke Johann]] (died 1859), who took over Styrian estates from 1818, was painted in grey Styrian [[loden-coat|loden]] by Leopold Kupelwieser in 1828, an image habsburger.net says entered Austrian memory. Vienna distrusted the dress, and in 1823 the Hofkanzlei banned state officials from wearing it. In Bavaria, an 1835 Oktoberfest parade marked the silver wedding of Ludwig I, and [[maximilian-ii-of-bavaria|Maximilian II]] had traditional dress accepted at court, writing in 1849 that it mattered for national feeling. In 1895 Maximilian Schmidt's Oktoberfest parade of about 1,400 people made the Alpine kit stand for German folk dress in general.

The first Trachtenverein, or costume club, is dated differently. Wikipedia's Tracht article gives Miesbach in 1859; its Lederhosen article gives an 1883 society founded by the teacher Joseph Vogl in the Leitzach Valley and Bayrischzell. They may be different clubs.

## The clothes

[[lederhosen|Lederhosen]] probably began as late-medieval work and hunting breeches; August Lewald first described the above-the-knee cut in [[tyrol|Tyrol]] in 1835. The Joanneum in Graz shows an Aussee man of about 1870 in Lederhosen, and notes a knee-baring version in fashion from about 1850. Austria-Forum says the grey, green-trimmed [[steireranzug|Steireranzug]] grew from the hunters' clothing of the Salzkammergut, Eisenwurzen and Mürzer Oberland, taking shape in Styria from the mid-19th century, with borrowings from uniform, and that [[franz-joseph-i|Franz Joseph]] wore it hunting. Its dates vary with what counts as the suit. The blog High Latitude Style describes the [[janker|Janker]] as a boxy, hip-length loden jacket with horn buttons.

## Shops and politics

Tracht was also a trade. [[lodenfrey|Lodenfrey]] says it was founded in Munich in 1842; Wikipedia says the firm profited from Aryanisation under the Nazis. The Jewish brothers Moritz and Julius Wallach opened their Volkskunsthaus in 1900, dressed the 1911 Oktoberfest and lost the business in 1938. Viktor von Geramb founded the Steirisches Heimatwerk in 1934, and Gertrud Pesendorfer designed a reduced "Germanic" dress for the Nazi state.
