Henry Poole & Co is usually counted the first tailor on [[savile-row|Savile Row]] itself. Much of its early history comes from the firm, so its dates are its own claims unless another source is named.

## To the Row

The firm says James Poole, a Shropshire linen draper, began in London in 1806, made military tunics with his wife Mary during the Napoleonic wars, and led London's tailors by 1815. Wikipedia places the start at Brunswick Square, and Mr Porter says the firm moved from there to the Row. In 1846, the London Museum says, it turned the workshops behind its shop on Old Burlington Street into a grand entrance on the Savile Row side. The London Museum says it is thought to be the first tailor on the Row; Mr Porter calls that generally agreed. It trades today from 15 Savile Row, though Mr Porter says it left the street from 1961 to 1982. Wikipedia says [[frederick-scholte|Frederick Scholte]]'s shop overlooked it.

The firm says James's son Henry inherited in 1846; Wikipedia says the move to the Row followed James's death. Henry ran it until he died in 1876, and his cousin [[samuel-cundey|Samuel Cundey]] (1823-1883) took over. The Cundey family has owned it since, five generations by Wikipedia's count, and the firm says Simon Cundey runs it now.

## Clients and warrants

The firm says [[napoleon-iii|Napoleon III]] gave Henry his first royal warrant in 1858 and Queen Victoria the next in 1869. The Bowes Museum exhibition page repeats both dates. Mr Porter says [[edward-vii|Edward VII]] was a customer from the early 1860s, as Prince of Wales, and that his circle, visiting royals and American millionaires made the firm's name. Its clients included [[winston-churchill|Winston Churchill]], Prince Rudolf of Austria, Lucius Beebe and Buffalo Bill. Wikipedia says it supplies court dress to the Lord Chamberlain's office and has a livery department.

Its order ledgers survive from 1846 to the 1950s: over 120 volumes by the firm's count, 126 of more than 1,000 pages each in Mr Porter's.

## The dinner jacket

The firm says it made the first dinner suit, for the Prince of Wales, in 1865. Wikipedia gives 1860 and says Poole is widely believed to have designed the first modern [[dinner-jacket|dinner jacket]], in the 1860s. Wikipedia says that in 1886 the Prince hosted James Potter of Tuxedo Park, New York, at [[sandringham|Sandringham]]; Potter had a jacket made at Poole and wore it at the Tuxedo Club, whose members copied it. *History Today* tells the same story but calls him James Potter Brown and puts the jacket's first outing at the club's Autumn Ball. No source here shows a document behind either, so treat both as the house story.
