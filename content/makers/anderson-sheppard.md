[[fred-astaire|Fred Astaire]] was a regular at Anderson & Sheppard from 1923, according to Footprints of London, and the house kept a client list to match: Noël Coward, George Gershwin, Cole Porter, Laurence Olivier, and Marlene Dietrich, its most famous woman client. Woolmark adds Gary Cooper, Cary Grant, Picasso and the [[duke-of-windsor|Duke of Windsor]]. Woolmark itself calls the story of Astaire testing his clothes in the fitting-room mirrors folklore.

## Founding

Woolmark says Per Anderson founded the firm in 1906 at 30 [[savile-row|Savile Row]]. Anderson had trained under [[frederick-scholte|Frederick Scholte]]; Footprints calls Scholte a young radical and Anderson a Swede, the only source to give his nationality. Anderson then joined the trouser cutter Sidney Sheppard. Square Mile says Anderson took the cutting method Scholte had developed.

Its sign read civil tailors, setting it apart from houses with military roots such as [[gieves-and-hawkes|Gieves & Hawkes]] at No. 1. Square Mile says it was not historically a royal tailor and was better known as tailor to the stars. It now holds a Royal Warrant as tailors from the King. New customers once needed a reference from an existing client.

## The cut

The house cut is the [[drape-cut|English drape]]. Woolmark describes a [[natural-shoulder|soft, rounded shoulder]] with minimal padding, a high [[armhole]], very light [[canvas]], hand-drawn lapels proportioned to the chest, and pleated trousers with side tabs; the person who measures a customer also cuts the pattern. Footprints adds generous cloth over the shoulder blades and chest and a roomy upper sleeve. Square Mile repeats the story that the drape was inspired by Guards officers' tunics, and says other tailors copied it without growing to a similar size.

## Owners and shops

Woolmark says Lonrho bought the company in the late 1970s, under Roland "Tiny" Rowland, and that his daughter Anda Rowland left Christian Dior in 2004 to run it; Wikipedia says she has owned it since 2004. In March 2005 the firm moved about 100 yards to 32 Old Burlington Street, where the workrooms are. Wikipedia says Tom Ford has been a customer since 2004. Square Mile says the Prince of Wales, a customer for more than twenty years, first visited the shop in December 2012.

The Haberdashery, a second shop round the corner at 19 Clifford Street, opened in late 2012 according to Woolmark. It began with casual trousers for clients and now also sells knitwear and accessories.
