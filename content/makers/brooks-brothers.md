Henry Sands Brooks opened H. & D. H. Brooks & Co. on 7 April 1818, on the corner of Catherine and Cherry Streets in Manhattan. From 1849 the firm sold suits made up in advance rather than to order, and it did much to create the ready-to-wear market in America. The Golden Fleece, a sheep hanging from a ribbon long used by British wool merchants, became its trademark in 1850.

Abraham Lincoln wore a Brooks frock coat to his second inauguration in 1865 and had it on the night he was shot. Its lining carried a hand-stitched eagle and the words "One Country, One Destiny".

## What Brooks introduced

The firm's list of firsts reads like an index of the American classic wardrobe. In 1895 came the unstructured [[sack-suit]] that the Ivy League would later wear. In 1896, according to the firm, John Brooks, the founder's grandson, put the [[button-down-collar]] on its shirts, the beginning of the [[ocbd]]. Madras followed in 1902, the Shetland sweater in 1904, Harris Tweed in 1909 and the polo coat around 1910. In the early 1930s it made lightweight summer suits in corduroy and seersucker, in 1953 a wash-and-wear shirt blended with Dacron that it called Brooksweave, and in 1957 it became the first American retailer to make argyle socks for men.

Together with [[j-press|J. Press]], Brooks was the main source of suits for the [[ivy-league-look]], and after the Second World War its button-down was the most popular shirt style in the country.

## Later years

In 1998 Brooks launched a non-iron dress shirt in pure cotton. In July 2020, hit by the pandemic, it filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, and in September a joint venture of Authentic Brands Group and Simon Property Group bought it. Its parent was SPARC Group until 2025 and Catalyst Brands after that.
