René Lacoste found tennis whites of the 1920s, a long-sleeved shirt, long trousers and a tie, too restrictive, and played in a short-sleeved knitted shirt instead. Smithsonian says he credited the idea to his friend the Marquis of Cholmondeley, who wore a [[polo]] shirt on court, and said an English tailor made him shirts. In a 1970 interview reported by Openers, he said he caught colds in a baggy shirt and took the soft, short-sleeved, collarless shirts of polo players and had a shirtmaker add a collar.

## Champion

He was born in Paris on 2 July 1904 and died at Saint-Jean-de-Luz on 12 October 1996. He won the French championships in 1925, 1927 and 1929, Wimbledon in 1925 and 1928 and the U.S. championships in 1926 and 1927, and the Davis Cup with France in 1927 and 1928, as one of the [[four-musketeers|Four Musketeers]]. His methodical baseline game also earned him the name "Tennis Machine". He withdrew from top-level [[tennis]] in 1929, with a brief return at the 1932 French championships, and entered the International Tennis Hall of Fame in 1976.

## The shirt

The sources do not agree on when he first wore it. Smithsonian says the 1926 U.S. championships, Openers 1927 on an American court, and the introduction to Wikipedia's article 1929. Wikipedia, Openers and histoiredutennis give 1933 for the business: with André Gillier, head of a large French knitwear firm, he founded La Société Chemise [[lacoste|Lacoste]], which Openers places in Troyes. Openers names the shirt model L1212. New World Encyclopedia calls the cloth a jersey knit; the atlas's [[polo-shirt]] entry, on other sources, gives [[pique|piqué]]. Smithsonian says the shirt reached the United States in 1952 under licence to [[izod|Izod]], whose Vincent Draddy handed shirts to public figures, and that the two firms parted in 1993.

## The crocodile

His friend Robert George drew a crocodile that was put on his blazer. Why a crocodile is a story told several ways. Smithsonian, citing *GQ*, says American papers called him the Alligator in 1927 after a bet of an alligator-skin suitcase with the French Davis Cup captain; histoiredutennis puts the same bet in 1926, and Openers tells a 1925 Boston anecdote instead. Wikipedia marks the suitcase story as needing a citation, and Smithsonian adds that some put the name down to his pugnacity or his pointed nose.

## Later

He married the golfer Simone de la Chaume in 1930; their daughter Catherine became a golf champion. He invented a ball-throwing practice machine and patented a steel tubular racket in 1961, which Wilson sold in America. His son Bernard took over the firm in 1963 according to Wikipedia, 1964 according to New World Encyclopedia.
