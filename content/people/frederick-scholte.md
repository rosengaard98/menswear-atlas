For nearly thirty years Frederick Scholte cut the Prince of Wales's coats, and he kept so low a profile that, by [[anderson-sheppard|Anderson & Sheppard]]'s account, he was reluctant to take new customers even from the royal circle.

He was born Friedrich Petrus Scholte in Amsterdam on 19 July 1865, came to London in the 1880s and was naturalised in 1899, sponsored by Viscount Ridley. He married Emma Lewellen in 1888 and they had four children; their son Owen, a flying ace, died in 1918. Scholte died on 2 December 1948 at Holloway Sanatorium, Virginia Water, Surrey. All of this comes from Wikipedia, which cites the naturalisation record and the probate calendar.

## On the Row

Anderson & Sheppard says he began working at 7 [[savile-row|Savile Row]] around 1900, a date that is the firm's own claim. Wikipedia says his shop overlooked [[henry-poole|Henry Poole & Co]]. In 1917 the *[[tailor-and-cutter|Tailor & Cutter]]* gave him an article, "The Scholte Lounge", which called his firm one that was making history; this is known here only as Wikipedia quotes it.

## The drape

Anderson & Sheppard, Put This On, *The Rake* and Wikipedia all credit him with the London cut or English [[drape-cut|drape]], and that is the usual attribution rather than a settled fact. Derek Guy at Put This On describes more room over the chest and shoulder blades, soft folds falling from the collarbone, high armholes and unpadded shoulders. *The Rake* adds a small, high armhole, extra fullness in the sleevehead and a [[natural-shoulder|natural shoulder]]. Only Wikipedia says he was apprenticed with the Household Cavalry and took the wide shoulders, roomy armholes and narrow waist of their uniform into his cut.

The dates do not agree. Anderson & Sheppard puts his work on the cut around 1900, while Wikipedia dates the drape to the 1930s. Per Anderson trained under him, says Put This On, and founded his own firm in 1906 as Anderson & Simmons; it took the name Anderson & Sheppard after Simmons sold his stake to the trouser cutter Sidney Sheppard.

## The Prince of Wales

Wikipedia says Scholte was principal tailor to the Prince of Wales, the future [[duke-of-windsor|Duke of Windsor]], from 1919 until his death, and Anderson & Sheppard says the Prince admired the new style. The Met holds a 1936 [[morning-coat|morning suit]] with a jacket by F. Scholte, trousers by Forster & Son and a label reading "H.M. The King, 22.6.36", and an [[evening-dress|evening suit]] dated 1938-65 made with the New York tailor H. Harris. Historic Royal Palaces has a green Lord of the Isles tartan suit with a Scholte jacket, worn by the Duke as early as the 1960s. Both of those dates run past Scholte's death, and the sources do not say who cut the later pieces.
