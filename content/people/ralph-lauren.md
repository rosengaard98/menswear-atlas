Ralph Lauren started with neckties. In 1967 he began selling wide ties of his own under the name Polo, made by the tie firm Beau Brummell. EBSCO puts them at about four inches across, when early-1960s ties ran to about two and a half. EBSCO and *Social Life* give first-year sales of about $500,000.

## Early years

He was born Ralph Lifshitz in the Bronx on 14 October 1939. He and his brother Jerry changed the family name to Lauren as teenagers: at sixteen, say Biography.com and EBSCO, or in 1955, says *Social Life*. He studied business at Baruch College, served in the Army and sold clothes at [[brooks-brothers|Brooks Brothers]] before moving into neckwear at Rivetz and then Beau Brummell.

## Polo

[[bloomingdales|Bloomingdale's]] first wanted the ties under a store label (Sutton East, in *Social Life*'s account) or narrower (in EBSCO's), then took them on his terms. The start-up money is disputed: $30,000 according to Biography.com, $50,000 from [[norman-hilton|Norman Hilton]] according to EBSCO. A full menswear collection followed in 1968. The company says Bloomingdale's gave him its first single-designer boutique in 1970, and that in 1971 he opened a store on Rodeo Drive, the first by an American designer in its own claim, along with women's shirts carrying an embroidered pony. In 1972 came a short-sleeved cotton mesh [[polo-shirt|polo shirt]] in 24 colours with the polo-player emblem, which became his signature piece.

## Films and shapes

For *[[the-great-gatsby-1974|The Great Gatsby]]* (1974) the company and Gentleman's Gazette say the period clothes came from his Polo line, except [[robert-redford|Robert Redford]]'s pink suit, which he designed. EBSCO says he designed Redford's whole wardrobe. In *[[annie-hall|Annie Hall]]* (1977) [[diane-keaton|Diane Keaton]] wore pieces of his, and he says the look was her own. Gentleman's Gazette credits him with reviving 1930s shapes, such as wide ties and [[double-breasted-suit|double-breasted suits]], and with mixing tailoring and Western workwear.

## The business

On 21 April 1986 he opened a flagship in the Rhinelander mansion at Madison Avenue and 72nd Street, built in 1894-98 and a New York landmark since 1976. Purple Label, an English-leaning tailoring line, dates from 1994 or 1995, depending on which Gentleman's Gazette page you read. Its reviewer calls it "an American take on [[savile-row|Savile Row]]", soft and unstructured, once mostly made in England and now mostly in Italy. The company went public on 11 June 1997, under the symbol RL,, and in September 2015 he stepped down as chief executive, staying on as executive chairman and chief creative officer. Britannica describes the business as built around an "elite American lifestyle", sold through clothing, home and fragrance.
