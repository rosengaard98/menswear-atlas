Vincenzo Attolini's jacket is better recorded than the man. No source here gives his birth or death year. QuiFinanza says only that he stayed a master cutter until his death, and Permanent Style saw his bespoke pattern among the pictures on the family firm's walls.

## At London House

*The Rake* says he was apprenticed to [[salvatore-morziello|Salvatore Morziello]]. Parisian Gentleman and Sprezza Boutique make him head cutter at Gennaro Rubinacci's London House in Naples, the house later called [[rubinacci|Rubinacci]]. There he is credited with the soft [[neapolitan-tailoring|Neapolitan]] jacket, with padding and lining taken out. Parisian Gentleman says it could be folded eight times. *The Rake* tells it as a 1930s story: Gennaro "Bebè" Rubinacci proposed an even less structured coat and Attolini developed it with ideas from the Roman tailor [[domenico-caraceni|Domenico Caraceni]]. Parisian Gentleman calls Caraceni's part uncertain. Sprezza credits the two men together and calls the result a reworking of [[savile-row|Savile Row]], not a rejection of it. QuiFinanza says colleagues at first doubted a jacket that went against fashion, and calls him a founding father of Neapolitan tailoring.

## The dates

The dates do not fit together. The family firm dates itself to 1930, the year it says the young Vincenzo made a jacket with no padding, shoulder pads or lining, "as soft and lightweight as a shirt", in a workshop on Via Vetriera. That page does not mention London House or Rubinacci. Wikipedia puts the turn to lighter jackets in the 1920s and names [[caggiula|Antonio Caggiula]] beside him. London House opened in 1931 by QuiFinanza's account and 1932 by Wikipedia's and Sprezza's, which sits oddly with a 1920s or 1930 jacket made as its head cutter. No source says when, or whether, he left.

## The family

By the firm's own account its clients included Totò, De Sica, Mastroianni, Clark Gable, Vittorio Emanuele III and the [[duke-of-windsor|Duke of Windsor]], said to have admired a jacket he saw in Capri; QuiFinanza names De Sica, Mastroianni, Totò and the Duke too. Wikipedia says his son [[cesare-attolini|Cesare]] set up production at scale and the marketing, and *The Rake* dates Cesare's system of specialist craftsmen to the 1960s. Grandsons Giuseppe and Massimiliano now run the firm from Casalnuovo di Napoli. Massimiliano told *The Rake* his grandfather made the jacket more like a second skin, and the family still keeps bespoke patterns in cloth, as Vincenzo's team did. A grandson also named Vincenzo, Cesare's eldest son, founded Stile Latino; he is a different man.
