George Bryan Brummell was born in London on 7 June 1778 and died in Caen, France, on 30 March 1840. As a close friend of the Prince of Wales, the future George IV, he had enormous influence over what fashionable men in London wore.

## What he changed

Brummell preferred well-fitted bespoke tailoring to ornament, part of the shift historians call the [[great-male-renunciation|Great Male Renunciation]]. For daily dress he wore dark coats with full-length trousers instead of knee breeches and stockings. He and his tailor Jonathan Meyer are sometimes credited with the modern trouser, though that is unproven. His real signature was linen: immaculate shirts and an elaborately knotted [[cravat]]. His habits of bathing every day, shaving carefully and cleaning his teeth were copied by the upper classes.

## The fall

The friendship cooled after the Prince became Regent in 1811, and it ended in July 1813, when the Regent cut him at a masquerade at Watier's club. Deep in debt, Brummell fled to France in 1816 to escape debtors' prison.
