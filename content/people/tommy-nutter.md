*Punch*, quoted by Put This On, called his clothes a mix of Lord Emsworth, the Great Gatsby and Bozo the Clown. Tommy Nutter made them on [[savile-row|Savile Row]], and *The Rake* calls his shop the first new company on the Row in 100 years.

## Before the Row

Thomas Albert Nutter was born on 17 April 1943 in Barmouth, Wales, according to Wikipedia. He studied plumbing and architecture, then left at 19 to learn tailoring at the Tailor and Cutter Academy. He went to work at the Savile Row house Donaldson, Williamson & Ward, where Put This On says he was a salesman, and there he met the cutter [[edward-sexton|Edward Sexton]].

## Nutters of Savile Row

[[nutters-of-savile-row|Nutters of Savile Row]] opened at 35a Savile Row in 1969; *The Rake* gives St Valentine's Day. The backers were Cilla Black, her husband Bobby Willis, Peter Brown of the Beatles' management and the lawyer James Vallance-White. The shop dressed its windows instead of hiding behind a velvet curtain, and it cut for women from the start.

*The Rake* describes the house cut: long, waisted, flared jackets with narrow, square, roped shoulders, very wide [[peak-lapel|peak lapels]] even on single-breasted coats, and trousers with parallel legs. Mr Porter describes the signature as a long [[hacking-jacket]] shape with contrast trims and loud checks; Put This On adds a nipped waist and bias-cut patch pockets.

Three of [[the-beatles|the Beatles]] wore Nutters suits on the *Abbey Road* cover. John Lennon married Yoko Ono in a cream corduroy Nutter jacket, and the Jaggers wore Nutter clothes at their 1971 wedding. Mr Porter, Put This On and Wikipedia also name Elton John, Twiggy, Eric Clapton, Peter Sellers, David Hockney, Roy Strong and Diana Ross as clients.

## After 1976

In 1976 Sexton became leading shareholder and Nutter left. Wikipedia calls it a buy-out, Archived Dreams says Sexton removed him, and Put This On says only that they fell out. Nutter worked for [[kilgour|Kilgour, French & Stanbury]] and in ready-to-wear for Austin Reed, whose Cue Shop showed his first collection in 1979, per Archived Dreams. He came back as Tommy Nutter, Savile Row, at No. 19: in 1983 with J&J Crombie behind him according to Wikipedia, in 1982 with Alan Lewis according to Archived Dreams. Mr Porter says he dressed characters for *Batman* (1989), and Archived Dreams says he made Jack Nicholson's Joker suit; Wikipedia flags the claim as needing a citation.

He died on 17 August 1992, aged 49, of AIDS-related complications. Sexton's firm and Chittleborough & Morgan still cut refined versions of his shapes, and Put This On names [[richard-james|Richard James]], [[ozwald-boateng|Ozwald Boateng]] and Tom Ford among those he paved the way for. Lance Richardson's *House of Nutter* (2018) is the biography.
