The most influential dresser of the 1920s was the Prince of Wales, the future [[edward-viii|Edward VIII]], then the most photographed celebrity of his time. When he toured the United States in 1924, the trade paper *Men's Wear* noted that young American men cared more about his clothes than about anyone else's.

Sport kept moving into ordinary dress. [[rene-lacoste|René Lacoste]] wore his short-sleeved piqué tennis shirt at the U.S. championships in 1926, put a crocodile on it in 1927, and from 1933 sold it through his own company, the beginning of the modern [[polo-shirt]]. [[brooks-brothers]] made light summer suits in corduroy and seersucker in the early 1930s.

On elite American campuses the [[ivy-league-look]] was taking shape around the [[ocbd]]. In February 1926 *Men's Wear* took the trouble to print the measurements of a Brooks button-down, size fifteen and a half.
