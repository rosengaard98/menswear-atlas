Strictly, the Edwardian era is the reign of [[edward-vii|Edward VII]], from 1901 to 1910, but it is commonly stretched to the outbreak of the First World War in 1914.

In those years the sober, single-coloured suit replaced the black frock coat as a man's regular daywear, and it took the name [[lounge-suit]]. Evening and formal dress loosened too: the dinner suit and the black lounge suit known as the stroller appeared as less formal alternatives to white tie and morning dress.

Across the Atlantic, [[brooks-brothers]] was assembling the American classic wardrobe. Its unstructured [[sack-suit]] dated from 1895 and its button-down shirt from 1896. It introduced madras from India in 1902, the Shetland sweater in 1904 and Harris Tweed in 1909, with the polo coat following around 1910.
