The regency that gives the period its name lasted only from 1811 to 1820, while the future George IV ruled in place of his father. Historians usually stretch the label from around 1795 to Queen Victoria's accession in 1837.

In men's clothes the leading figure was [[beau-brummell|Beau Brummell]]. He dressed in dark coats and full-length trousers rather than knee breeches and stockings, and put his effort into fit and into perfect white linen and an elaborately knotted [[cravat]]. His preference for sober, well-cut cloth over ornament is counted as part of the [[great-male-renunciation|Great Male Renunciation]], the turn away from display in men's dress. The dark coat carried on into the [[victorian]] era.
