After the Second World War the button-down became the most popular shirt style in America, and the [[ivy-league-look]] it belonged to spread from campus to the office. Veterans studying on the G.I. Bill wore their khaki [[chinos]] to class in the late 1940s, and the trousers became part of the uniform. In 1957 and 1958 about 70 percent of all suits sold in the United States were in the Ivy style, and it stayed the standard for middle-class men until the mid-1960s.

Technology arrived in the wardrobe too. In 1953 [[brooks-brothers]] sold a wash-and-wear shirt in a blend of Dacron polyester and cotton, which it called Brooksweave, and in 1957 it became the first American retailer to make argyle socks for men.

The era closes with Ivy at its peak and about to fall. Japanese photographers shooting [[take-ivy]] captured the campus look just before the [[peacock-revolution]] swept it aside.
