Button a collar point down and something has to give. If the collar is stiff or the points are short, it simply lies flat. If the points are long and the collar is soft, the cloth bows outward between the band and the button, and the front edges of the collar form a curve. That curve is the roll.

The original Brooks collars had long points and no interlining at all, which gave them a soft and slightly uneven roll. Golden-era points measured about three and a half inches, and the [[button-down-collar|tip buttons]] were placed so that the collar would roll with what Put This On describes as an "angel wings" look once buttoned.

## Why it disappeared

Around the 1990s Brooks added an interlining to the collar. It made the collar better behaved and less wrinkled, and it took much of the roll away. Many modern button-downs are lined and have shorter points, and some show no roll at all when the tips are buttoned.

The argument is old. A 1983 *Esquire* piece, reprinted by Ivy Style, already treated a full, rounded roll as the only correct shape for a Brooks collar and dismissed short, straight collars as an oddity that hardly needed buttons.
