Oxford is a basket weave, a variation of plain weave. Instead of single threads crossing one by one, two or more weft yarns, or one heavier yarn, pass over and under two or more warp yarns at a time. Britannica gives oxford shirting as its example. The grouped threads give the cloth a faint checkerboard texture you can see up close.

## The name

The usual story is that a Scottish mill in the nineteenth century wove four shirtings and named them after universities: Oxford, Cambridge, Yale and Harvard. Only Oxford stayed in use. The story is repeated by shirtmakers but none of the tellings name the mill, so treat it as tradition rather than record. Either way the name was borrowed for prestige; the cloth has no real connection to the town.

## On the button-down

[[brooks-brothers]] moved its [[button-down-collar|polo collar]] onto white cotton cheviot, a cloth the trade knew as oxford. Until the early twentieth century [[polo|polo]] players themselves wore thick, long-sleeved shirts of oxford cotton.

Non-iron finishes changed that: Put This On called non-iron oxford the death knell of the original shirt's character.
