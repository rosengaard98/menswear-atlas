The loop has two origin stories. One points to sailors, who had almost no storage and used loops to hang shirts up to dry. The other, and the one that explains its place on the button-down, credits the shirtmaker [[gant|GANT]] with adding it in the 1960s so customers could hang their shirts in a locker without creasing them. Many of those customers were Ivy League students.

On campus it took on a second meaning. A missing loop was said to mean a man was spoken for, and partners took to tearing the loops off, which became a small fad in the 1960s. According to one account, women complained that some makers stitched their loops on too firmly to rip, and a mail-order company sold loose loops so nobody had to damage a shirt.

The loop is a later addition to the [[ocbd]]. Golden-era Brooks Brothers shirts had a plain [[box-pleat]] with no loop above it. Not everyone likes it now: ASKET points out that hanging a shirt by its loop strains the cloth and that a loop caught on a hook can tear off.
