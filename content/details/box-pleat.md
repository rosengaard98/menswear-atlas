A box pleat is made by folding the cloth twice, with one folded edge facing right and the other facing left, so a flat panel stands slightly proud between them. Merriam-Webster records the term from 1857.

On a shirt it is a way of storing extra width. The back is cut wider than the [[yoke]] it hangs from, and the surplus is folded into the pleat at the centre. When the wearer reaches forward the pleat opens, and at rest it closes so the back still hangs straight.

On golden-era Brooks Brothers button-downs the box pleat started just below the yoke and ran down to the waist, where it opened into the fullness of the shirttail. Those shirts had no [[locker-loop]] on top of the pleat. That came later and from elsewhere.
