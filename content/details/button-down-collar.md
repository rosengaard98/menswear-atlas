Late nineteenth-century [[polo|polo]] players in England fastened the points of their collars to their shirts so the collars would not flap about during play. [[john-e-brooks|John E. Brooks]] of [[brooks-brothers]] saw this at a match and had the idea copied in New York. Brooks Brothers gives the year as 1896; some accounts put his trip and the first sales in 1900.

It started as sportswear. Brooks's first soft-collar shirts were pullovers meant for games, and only later did the collar move onto a shirt that buttons all the way down the front, the [[ocbd]].

## The buttons

The buttons are small and placed with care, because where they sit decides how the collar curves. On golden-era Brooks shirts the tip buttons sat three quarters of an inch from the points and three and a quarter inches apart. That spacing dates from 1929. From the 1940s to the 1980s Brooks set them about half an inch further apart, and by 2013 Brooks had gone back to the 1929 spacing, on collar points about a quarter inch shorter.

Placed well, the buttons hold the points down without flattening the collar, so it bows outward in the [[collar-roll]] that enthusiasts look for. Some Brooks shirts also carried a third button at the centre back, the [[back-collar-button]].
