## Claims
- Zazous were a youth subculture of Vichy France, active about 1940 to 1944, mostly in Paris. Members were largely 17 to 20 and numbered in the hundreds, by Wikipedia's account — [Zazou (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazou)
- The scene was swing-jazz driven. Wikipedia says it also drew on British and American pop music and on the zoot suit — [Zazou (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazou)
- Two hangouts. The Pam Pam café terrace on the Champs-Élysées drew older, more middle-class zazous, who also cycled in the Bois de Boulogne. The "Boul'Mich" (Boulevard Saint-Michel, Latin Quarter) had cellar clubs such as the Dupont-Latin and the Capoulade — [Zazou (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazou), [The Zazous 1940-1945 (Organise! 59, Anarchist Federation, via libcom)](https://files.libcom.org/files/1940-1945%20The%20Zazous.pdf), [Zazous: What punk culture meant in World War II France (Stephanie Buck, Timeline/Medium, 4 Oct 2016)](https://medium.com/timeline/zazou-france-world-war-ii-9f26b36e0ee3)
- Men's look: extra-long jackets to the knee with many pockets and half-belts; narrow trousers gathered at the waist; narrow ties of cotton or heavy wool; high shirt collars held by a horizontal pin; thick-soled suede shoes with white or bright socks; long greased hair — [Zazou (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazou), [Organise! 59](https://files.libcom.org/files/1940-1945%20The%20Zazous.pdf)
- Umbrellas: furled, carried even in sun, nicknamed "Chamberlain" umbrellas (a parody of English style, per Buck and Organise!). Wikipedia says the umbrella read as British and the hair was worn "à la mode d'Oxford". Checks on jackets, skirts and umbrellas are also mentioned — [Buck, Timeline](https://medium.com/timeline/zazou-france-world-war-ii-9f26b36e0ee3), [Organise! 59](https://files.libcom.org/files/1940-1945%20The%20Zazous.pdf), [Zazou (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazou)
- Other male details: tiny moustaches and false collars (Buck); a different sketch has knee-length jackets, short trousers, string ties, hair long at the back and quiffed at the front, dark glasses (Peril) — [Buck](https://medium.com/timeline/zazou-france-world-war-ii-9f26b36e0ee3), [Zazous (Lynn Peril, Semiovox, 14 Oct 2026)](https://www.semiovox.com/articles/2026/10/14/zazous/)
- Women's look: wide-shouldered jackets, short pleated skirts, striped or net stockings, heavy wooden-soled shoes, bright red lipstick, sunglasses, blonde shoulder-length curls or braids — [Zazou (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazou), [Organise! 59](https://files.libcom.org/files/1940-1945%20The%20Zazous.pdf)
- Wikipedia says the oversized cut was a dig at wartime cloth rationing (the excess material mocked government decrees). Wikipedia quotes historian W.D. Halls (Wikipedia spells Hall) describing "drape suits", drainpipe trousers, sheepskin-lined jackets and brilliantined hair — [Zazou (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazou)
- Dior's autobiography, *Dior by Dior*, is cited on Wikipedia as calling the style a defiance of occupiers and austerity, and ugly as fashion ("Hats were far too large, skirts far too short"). I did not open Dior's book — [Zazou (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazou)
- Johnny Hess's "Je suis swing" is the zazous' anthem. Wikipedia says it came out in early 1942 and sold more than any earlier French record; Peril dates it 1938 (see Disagreements) — [Zazou (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazou), [Semiovox](https://www.semiovox.com/articles/2026/10/14/zazous/)
- Name: probably from Cab Calloway's song (Buck gives "Zah Zuh Zahby"; Peril gives "Zaz Zuh Zaz", 1933); Boris Vian, linked to the scene, liked "z" words — [Buck](https://medium.com/timeline/zazou-france-world-war-ii-9f26b36e0ee3), [Semiovox](https://www.semiovox.com/articles/2026/10/14/zazous/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazou)
- Jazz roots: interwar Montmartre had a Black American jazz scene, and Django Reinhardt played swing in Paris clubs — [Zazou (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazou), [Organise! 59](https://files.libcom.org/files/1940-1945%20The%20Zazous.pdf)
- Press and official reaction: Wikipedia counts anti-zazou articles at 78 (1940), 9 (1941), 38 (1943), calling them work-shy, decadent, "Judeo-Gaullist" shirkers (Wikipedia gives no citation for these figures). A fascist magazine mocked the male type as "Ultra Swing 1941" — [Zazou (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazou), [Organise! 59](https://files.libcom.org/files/1940-1945%20The%20Zazous.pdf)
- Named papers (per Buck): *La Gerbe* (25 June 1942) attacked American influence and jazz; *Gringoire* praised police raids on cafés; *Le Cri du Peuple* ran a drawing of a Vichy youth cutting a zazou's hair — [Buck, Timeline](https://medium.com/timeline/zazou-france-world-war-ii-9f26b36e0ee3)
- *Jeunesse*, the pro-Nazi JPF youth magazine, mocked zazous as inferior (Peril). Wikipedia says the Jeunesse Populaire Française made them "Enemy Number One", squads with clippers shaving heads under "Scalp the Zazous!" — [Semiovox](https://www.semiovox.com/articles/2026/10/14/zazous/), [Zazou (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazou)
- Hair: a 27 March 1942 decree asked barbers to give hair to the war effort and zazous refused, growing it longer (Buck). Organise! dates a hair-for-slippers decree to 1942 — [Buck](https://medium.com/timeline/zazou-france-world-war-ii-9f26b36e0ee3), [Organise! 59](https://files.libcom.org/files/1940-1945%20The%20Zazous.pdf)
- July 1942: café raids, zazous sent to countryside work camps (Buck). Forced labour in 1942-43 is the point historians see the scene fading. Peril: police raids, arrests, harvest work, later labour camps — [Buck](https://medium.com/timeline/zazou-france-world-war-ii-9f26b36e0ee3), [Semiovox](https://www.semiovox.com/articles/2026/10/14/zazous/)
- Yellow star protest: after the June 1942 edict, some non-Jewish zazous wore stars lettered "zazou", "swing" or "goy" — [Buck](https://medium.com/timeline/zazou-france-world-war-ii-9f26b36e0ee3), [Semiovox](https://www.semiovox.com/articles/2026/10/14/zazous/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazou)
- Who they were: educated, often well-off, aged 17 to 20 (Sarah Fishman, quoted by Buck) — [Buck](https://medium.com/timeline/zazou-france-world-war-ii-9f26b36e0ee3)
- Historian David Drake, quoted by Peril, calls them "rebels not resisters". Organise! says the Communist resistance saw them as apathetic. A UW-Madison seminar abstract (Kelly Jakes, 2013) argues they were a subversive swing identity set against Vichy racial ideology — [Semiovox](https://www.semiovox.com/articles/2026/10/14/zazous/), [Organise! 59](https://files.libcom.org/files/1940-1945%20The%20Zazous.pdf), [Zazous in Zoot Suits (UW-Madison IRH, seminar 30 Sep 2013)](https://irh.wisc.edu/event/zazous-in-zoot-suits-race-jazz-and-resistance-in-occupied-france-1940-1944/)
- 1944: singer Andrex's song about zazous made them popular culture (Buck). 1986: Pet Shop Boys' "In the Night" is zazou-themed (Wikipedia) — [Buck](https://medium.com/timeline/zazou-france-world-war-ii-9f26b36e0ee3), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazou)
- Parallels: Swingjugend (Germany), Edelweiss Pirates, Czech potápky, Soviet stilyagi — [Zazou (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazou)

## Disagreements and myths
- Date of "Je suis swing": early 1942 (Wikipedia) vs 1938 (Peril). Unresolved; avoid a year or say sources differ.
- Calloway song title: "Zah Zuh Zahby" (Buck) vs "Zaz Zuh Zaz", 1933 (Peril). Name origin is "probably" in all sources.
- Zoot-suit link is asserted, not demonstrated. Wikipedia's source is a jazz-biography book; Peril hedges. The zoot suit of 1939-43 is American and mostly known in Paris through films and records. Do not state that zazous copied zoot suits.
- Umbrella: "Chamberlain" parody (Buck, Organise!) vs plain British signal (Wikipedia, Peril).
- Oversized cloth as protest at rationing is Wikipedia's reading; no primary statement from zazous found.
- Scale: Wikipedia says hundreds; popular articles imply a movement. Peril and Drake treat them as rebels rather than resisters; the UW abstract frames them as resistance.
- The anti-zazou press counts and the "Ultra Swing 1941" quote have no inline citation on Wikipedia. Treat as repeated, not verified.
- Wikipedia spells the historian "Hall"; the book is W.D. Halls, *The Youth of Vichy France* (Oxford, 1981).

## Construction notes (garments and details)
- Jacket: knee-length (long, single-breasted shape not stated in sources), many pockets, half-belt at the back or waist, wide shoulders (women's jackets stated; men's drape shoulders only implied). Illustrator should work from "drape" cut plus extra length.
- Trousers: narrow, gathered or pleated at the waist, "drainpipe"; ankle length not stated.
- Collar: high, with a horizontal collar bar; narrow tie.
- Shoes: thick-soled suede; white or coloured socks showing.
- Props: furled umbrella, sometimes checked.
- No measurements in any source opened. A drawing would be conjecture beyond these points; the Commons "typical zazou men's suit" drawing exists but is not PD (see below).
