# Madras (detail)

## Claims
- Madras is a lightweight handwoven cotton, usually in checks or plaids, named after Madras (now Chennai), Tamil Nadu. Authentic cloth looks the same on both sides and has slubs from short-staple yarn — [Wikipedia: Madras (cloth)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras_(cloth))
- The East India Company's Fort St George and Madras were established in 1639 after Francis Day's 1637 expedition to Madraspatnam; Armagon cloth was too poor to export — [Heddels](https://www.heddels.com/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/108332)
- Francis Day chose the site partly for cloth: Madraspatnam fabric was about 15 per cent cheaper than Armagaon's; historian Sriram is quoted that Madras was founded for cloth — [Madras Musings: Madras Checks](https://madrasmusings.com/vol-25-no-20/madras-checks/)
- The Company is said to have offered weavers a 30-year duty exemption; nearly 400 weaver families settled within a year — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras_(cloth))
- Checked cloth may predate the British: textile scholar Jasleen Dhamija cites 16th-century Portuguese records of checked scarves in the region — [Madras Musings](https://madrasmusings.com/vol-25-no-20/madras-checks/)
- The export cloth was the "Real Madras Handkerchief" (RMHK): 8-metre bolts, 36 inches wide, cut into three square kerchiefs, woven on a 60x40 loom; also called George cloth, Guinea cloth, Injiri — [Madras Musings](https://madrasmusings.com/vol-25-no-20/madras-checks/)
- Dyes: indigo warp, turmeric yellow; where they met, green ran into the weft when wet, hence "bleeding" — [Madras Musings](https://madrasmusings.com/vol-25-no-20/madras-checks/). Outlook Traveller lists plant colours rust red, indigo and a yellow-white mix — [Outlook Traveller](https://www.outlooktraveller.com/explore/story/73446/a-shirt-called-madras)
- Madras-check cottons were a major part of British trade with West Africa and travelled with the slave trade to the West Indies (V&A gallery label, handkerchief c.1855, made in Mangalore by the German Mission) — [V&A](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O480686/madras-handkerchief). Kalabari of Nigeria have traded in it about 400 years — [Madras Musings](https://madrasmusings.com/vol-25-no-20/madras-checks/)
- Plaids dominated by 1822, a trend linked to George IV's Scotland visit; Outlook Traveller instead says weavers copied tartans of Scottish regiments in Madras — [Heddels](https://www.heddels.com/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/108332), [Outlook Traveller](https://www.outlooktraveller.com/explore/story/73446/a-shirt-called-madras)
- US: Heddels says Elihu Yale, governor of Madras, gave madras yardage with other gifts to the Collegiate School in 1718 (Wikipedia repeats this as 1718). A 1897 Sears catalogue is early evidence; a 1919 NYT piece reported a shortage; 1930s-40s young men wore it as Bahamas holiday wear; *Gentry* featured it in 1953 — [Heddels](https://www.heddels.com/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/108332)
- 1958: importer William Jacobson sold Brooks Brothers 10,000 yards of vegetable-dyed Indian madras; the washing warning did not reach customers, the dye bled, complaints followed. *Seventeen* ran a feature "Bleeding Madras", with "guaranteed to bleed" captions, and orders flooded in — [Ivy Style (Schneider's account)](https://www.ivy-style.com/spin-cycle-how-bleeding-madras-washed-vice-into-virtue.html), [Heddels](https://www.heddels.com/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/108332), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras_(cloth))
- David Ogilvy used "guaranteed to bleed" for Hathaway madras shirts in the 1960s — [Ivy Style](https://www.ivy-style.com/spin-cycle-how-bleeding-madras-washed-vice-into-virtue.html), [Outlook Traveller](https://www.outlooktraveller.com/explore/story/73446/a-shirt-called-madras)
- Peak popularity in the 1960s, then machine-made imitations from Japan, Switzerland, England and France; Nigerian import bans in the 1970s collapsed the RMHK market — [Madras Musings](https://madrasmusings.com/vol-25-no-20/madras-checks/)
- Commenter recollection (unsourced, anecdotal): 1962 shirts were loose-weave and faded within months; by 1968 tighter and more colourfast — [Ivy Style comments](https://www.ivy-style.com/spin-cycle-how-bleeding-madras-washed-vice-into-virtue.html)
- J. Press madras jackets and Miyuki-zoku madras (1964) are already in atlas entries; I did not open new pages for those.

## Disagreements and myths
- Importer name: Ivy Style/Schneider says Jacobson bought from a "Mr. Nair"; Madras Musings says Capt. C.P. Nair of the Leela Group; Wikipedia says exporter Captain C.P. Krishnan. A Business Standard piece quoted in comments suggests the importer's account may be boastful.
- Who bought: Madras Musings says Seventeen later ordered a million yards; Ivy Style says millions of yards were eventually exported.
- Earlier US dates conflict: Yale 1718 (Heddels, Wikipedia), Sears 1897, a commenter claims Brooks introduced it in 1902 (unverified), another claims bleeding madras sold in Truman years (unsourced). The Yale gift is a story told in modern web articles, not checked against primary sources.
- Name: Wikipedia credits shirtmaker David J. Anderson with naming it in 1844 while noting earlier use.
- Tartan origin: George IV visit (Heddels, Madras Musings) vs regiments (Outlook Traveller).
- Outlook's "12th century gada" export claim is uncorroborated.

## Construction notes (garments and details)
- Cloth, not garment: yarn-dyed plaid/check, plain weave, short-staple carded cotton with slubs, same on both sides. Traditional colours indigo, turmeric yellow, red; green appears where they overlap. Uses: shirts, jackets, Bermuda shorts, ties, trousers. Illustrator can draw a plaid swatch with irregular stripe widths.
