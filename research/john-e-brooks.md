# John E. Brooks (person)

Verdict: too thin for its own entry. Everything documented in the pages opened is the polo/button-down story, and the sources disagree on the date. Recommend folding into brooks-brothers and button-down-collar (keep the existing [[john-e-brooks]] mentions as plain text or point them at those pages).

## Claims
- He was a grandson of Henry Sands Brooks, founder of Brooks Brothers. The source gives his dates only as "fl. late nineteenth century". — [EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/brooks-brothers-introduces-button-down-shirts)
- On a trip to England he watched a polo match, noticed the players buttoned their collar points down, and adapted the idea into a cotton shirt with a narrow rolled collar. EBSCO dates the trip to 1900 and first sale to fall 1900 at the New York flagship. — [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/brooks-brothers-introduces-button-down-shirts)
- He is said to have taken the design to the company directors, who approved it. EBSCO adds that he had an eye for profitable designs. — [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/brooks-brothers-introduces-button-down-shirts)
- Ivy Style calls him "Mr. John Brooks", who had retired as president in 1896. He vacationed in England in 1900, saw the buttoned collars, sent a shirt home, and the firm still calls it its "polo" collar. (Does not use the middle initial.) — [Ivy Style, Boyer on Brooks, 1981](https://www.ivy-style.com/?p=249)
- The Rake dates the button-down to 1896, calls him an American, says he brought the idea to "his grandfather's company", and gives no formal position. — [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/history-of-the-button-down-shirt)
- Company history lists the family line: Henry Sands Brooks d. 1833; sons ran it, name Brooks Brothers by 1850; Daniel retired 1879; Winthrop H. Brooks, great-grandson, stepped down as president in 1946 when the company was sold. It does not mention John E. Brooks, and gives 1900 for the button-down as the company's own account. — [Company-Histories.com](https://company-histories.com/Brooks-Brothers-Inc-Company-History.html)

## Disagreements and myths
- Date: 1896 (The Rake; also in the existing atlas entries) vs 1900 (EBSCO body text, Ivy Style, Company-Histories). EBSCO's own metadata shows both. 1896 is also the year Ivy Style says Brooks retired as president, which fits poorly with a 1900 trip but not with a visit by a retired man. Unresolved.
- Identity: the "John E." form appears only in EBSCO. Ivy Style says "Mr. John Brooks"; The Rake gives no name form in what was read. Whether he was ever a partner or president is stated only by Ivy Style (retired as president 1896); unconfirmed elsewhere.
- Existing atlas pages say he "was the founder's grandson" and "watched polo players in England" as fact. This is the company's own account; The Rake notes the story is uncited and the "polo" label is marketing. No independent primary source found.
- Nothing found on birth, death, parents, career, other products.
