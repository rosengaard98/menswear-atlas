# Spalla camicia (detail)

## Claims
- Literally "shirt shoulder" or "shirt sleeve": the Neapolitan way of joining sleeve to jacket body so the sleeve looks as if it runs under the shoulder, as on a shirt. — [Video: What 'spalla camicia' really means](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2019/12/video-what-spalla-camicia-really-means.html)
- Crompton stresses it is only about the join. It does not by itself mean a small shoulder, a natural shoulder, or extra gathering at the sleeve head. — same
- In a later comment he separates "camicia" (the join) from "mappina" (the amount of shirring at the sleeve top); a jacket can have either, both or neither, and tailors often assume shirring when they say camicia. — same (reader comment thread, not the article body)
- Cut: the sleeve head is cut larger than the armhole and the excess is eased in, giving small puckers (repecchie). The upper sleeve head (tromba) is wider than the lower part (scalfo). — [Why Spalla Camicia is the Hallmark of Neapolitan Tailoring](https://thevou.com/blog/spalla-camicia-guide-men/)
- Wikipedia says the sleeve is about 10 cm larger than the armhole, the extra gathered into the repecchia, giving the "manica a mappina" flare. — [Neapolitan tailoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_tailoring)
- Seam allowance is turned toward the shoulder rather than the sleeve, as in shirtmaking; no shoulder padding; sleeve head puckers slightly. — [The Italian Tailor, Naples, and the Neapolitan Jacket](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/the-italian-tailor-naples-and-the-neapolitan-jacket)
- The Rake: a much larger sleeve head is hand-stitched into a snug armhole, giving gathering called grinze, presented as prized, not a flaw. — [The History and Anatomy of Neapolitan Tailoring](https://therake.com/stories/the-history-and-anatomy-of-neapolitan-tailoring)
- Styleforum primer: unpadded natural shoulder, sleevehead allowances pressed toward the body, fabric gathered beneath the shoulder seam. — [Primer: Neapolitan Jacket Shoulder Style](https://journal.styleforum.net/tag/spalla-camicia)
- Variant "con rollino": a roll of wadding or heat-shaped cloth lifts the sleeve head for a rounder, roped line with less shirring. Wikipedia ties it to formal jackets. — The Rake; Wikipedia; Styleforum primer (above)
- The Neapolitan jacket generally: no shoulder pads, shoulder follows the body's natural curve. — Wikipedia (above)
- Origin story: Vincenzo Attolini, head cutter at Rubinacci's London House in Naples, stripped out pads and linings, moving away from British structure. — Parisian Gentleman (above)

## Disagreements and myths
- Meaning: Permanent Style says camicia is only the join; the Wikipedia, Vou and Rake accounts treat shirring as part of it. Crompton notes tailors themselves usually assume shirring.
- Attribution and date: Wikipedia puts Caggiula and Vincenzo Attolini rejecting the British model in the 1920s; The Rake gives the 1930s with Gennaro Rubinacci prompting Attolini (told as received history, not documented); Parisian Gentleman says Caraceni's influence on Attolini "cannot be confirmed". Treat all as stories. None of the pages dates the shoulder itself.
- The Rake's 14th-century guild lineage is narrative, not documented.
- Pleats as flaw vs feature: The Vou and The Rake call the puckers deliberate; the English coat's small puckers are noted differently in drape-cut.
- Terms vary: repecchia/repecchie (Wikipedia, Vou), grinze (Rake), mappina (Crompton, Wikipedia).
- Styleforum primer has no stated author or date (text suggests Nicola Radano).

## Construction notes (garments and details)
- Illustrator: a crop of the shoulder and upper sleeve, front view of a jacket, with the armhole seam sitting at the shoulder point and a few small gathers (fold class) at the top of the sleeve head, fewer toward the front. No shoulder pad; the shoulder line slopes softly.
- Pieces: jacket body, sleeve with an oversized head (sources give about 10 cm extra, Wikipedia only; unverified elsewhere).
- Stitching: sleeve sewn under the shoulder then fixed by hand on the outside; allowances turned toward the body.
- Variant with rollino: slightly raised, rounded roll at the sleeve head.
