# René Lacoste (person)

## Claims
- Born 2 July 1904 in Paris, died 12 October 1996 in Saint-Jean-de-Luz, aged 92. — [René Lacoste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Lacoste); same dates in [René Lacoste, histoiredutennis](https://histoiredutennis.com/champions/lacoste/E-lacoste.html) and [René Lacoste, New World Encyclopedia](https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Ren%C3%A9_Lacoste)
- Seven Grand Slam singles titles: French 1925, 1927, 1929; Wimbledon 1925, 1928; U.S. 1926, 1927. Davis Cup with France 1927 and 1928. Doubles titles with Jean Borotra. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Lacoste), [histoiredutennis](https://histoiredutennis.com/champions/lacoste/E-lacoste.html)
- One of the "Four Musketeers" (with Borotra, Brugnon, Cochet); inducted into the International Tennis Hall of Fame in 1976. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Lacoste)
- Nicknamed "the Crocodile"; also "Tennis Machine" for his methodical baseline game. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Lacoste)
- Found the long-sleeved shirt, long trousers and tie of tennis whites restrictive and first wore his short-sleeved shirt at the 1926 U.S. Open; he credited a friend, the Marquis of Cholmondeley, who wore a polo shirt on court, and said an English tailor made him shirts (quoted from a 1979 People article). — [The Story Behind the Lacoste Crocodile Shirt](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/blogs/the-story-behind-the-lacoste-crocodile-shirt-91276898/)
- In a 1970 newspaper interview (as reported by Openers) he said he caught colds in a baggy shirt and adapted the collared short-sleeved shirts of polo players; the shirt is the "L1212". — [LACOSTE: A Legacy in Sportswear](https://openers.jp/en/fashion/fashion_men/14478)
- A friend, Robert George, embroidered/drew a crocodile on his blazer. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Lacoste), [histoiredutennis](https://histoiredutennis.com/champions/lacoste/E-lacoste.html)
- In 1933 he and André Gillier, head of a large French knitwear/hosiery firm, founded La Société Chemise Lacoste to make the shirt with the crocodile on the chest; Openers places it in Troyes. — [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/blogs/the-story-behind-the-lacoste-crocodile-shirt-91276898/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Lacoste), [Openers](https://openers.jp/en/fashion/fashion_men/14478)
- The shirt reached the U.S. in 1952 (Smithsonian), licensed to Izod; Vincent Draddy gave shirts to public figures. Lacoste and Izod parted in 1993. — [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/blogs/the-story-behind-the-lacoste-crocodile-shirt-91276898/). Histoiredutennis says the brand went international in 1952 (Italy, U.S.).
- Married golfer Simone de la Chaume on 30 June 1930; their daughter Catherine Lacoste is a golf champion. Son Bernard took over the firm in 1963 (Wikipedia) or 1964 (New World Encyclopedia). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Lacoste)
- Inventor: a ball-throwing practice machine (histoiredutennis dates it 1927) and a tubular steel racket patented in 1961, marketed in the U.S. by Wilson and used by Billie Jean King and Jimmy Connors. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Lacoste), [histoiredutennis](https://histoiredutennis.com/champions/lacoste/E-lacoste.html)
- Retired from top play around 1930 (Openers) / early 1930s (Smithsonian). — [Openers](https://openers.jp/en/fashion/fashion_men/14478)

## Disagreements and myths
- Year of the shirt: 1926 U.S. Open (Smithsonian; polo-shirt entry), 1927 on a U.S. court (Openers), 1929 (Wikipedia's introduction "introduced the tennis shirt in 1929"), 1933 for the product launch (Openers, New World Encyclopedia, Wikipedia on company). Roland-Garros 1928 is cited in the existing entry. Treat the first appearance as 1926 to 1929 depending on source and 1933 as the commercial start.
- Crocodile origin is a story, told differently each time. Smithsonian (citing GQ): American press called him "Alligator" in 1927 after a wager of an alligator-skin suitcase with the French Davis Cup captain. Histoiredutennis: nickname from American press in 1926 after the bet with the captain (unnamed). Openers: a 1925 Boston anecdote with an Australian, Anderson, and a journalist George Carenns. Wikipedia marks the suitcase story "citation needed". Other suggested causes: his pugnacity, his pointed nose (Smithsonian, New World Encyclopedia).
- Crocodile versus alligator: American press said alligator; the emblem is a crocodile (Wikipedia, also "citation needed").
- Smithsonian also mentions an unattributed theory that the shirt came from British polo players in 19th-century India; that is not Lacoste's account.
- Bernard Lacoste took over in 1963 (Wikipedia) or 1964 (New World Encyclopedia).
- Rankings as world number one (1926, 1927, 1929) vary by source (Wikipedia).
- Histoiredutennis says the 1929 French win was his last official match, then that he played again in 1932; internally inconsistent. Its claim that his racket won 46 Slams is the page's own and unchecked.
- The existing polo-shirt entry says 1926 and 1933 and the Izod date as 1951 or 1952; this matches the sources except that Wikipedia's 1929 differs.
