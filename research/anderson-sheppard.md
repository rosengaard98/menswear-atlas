# Anderson & Sheppard (maker)

## Claims
- Savile Row bespoke house, founded 1906 by Per Anderson, a former apprentice of Frederick Scholte; the first premises were at 30 Savile Row (Woolmark says this; the 1906 date is the firm's own claim as repeated by others) — [Off the Row: Anderson & Sheppard](https://www.woolmark.com/z.archive/archive-industry/suiting/off-the-row-anderson-sheppard/)
- Footprints of London calls Scholte a Dutch "young radical" and says he trained Per Anderson, described there as Swedish; Anderson then joined trouser cutter Sidney Sheppard — [The Great Tailors of London](https://footprintsoflondon.com/2015/06/the-great-tailors-of-london-anderson-and-sheppard)
- Square Mile says Anderson took the cutting method from Scholte, who first developed it — [Britain's finest craft brands](https://squaremile.com/culture/britains-finest-craft-brands/)
- Moved in March 2005 about 100 yards to 32 Old Burlington Street, where the bespoke workrooms are — [Woolmark](https://www.woolmark.com/z.archive/archive-industry/suiting/off-the-row-anderson-sheppard/); Wikipedia also gives 2005 — [Anderson & Sheppard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_%26_Sheppard)
- Lonrho bought the company in the late 1970s under Roland "Tiny" Rowland; his daughter Anda Rowland left Christian Dior in 2004 to run it (Wikipedia says she has owned it since 2004, inherited from her father) — [Woolmark](https://www.woolmark.com/z.archive/archive-industry/suiting/off-the-row-anderson-sheppard/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_%26_Sheppard)
- The Haberdashery, a second shop at 19 Clifford Street, round the corner; Woolmark says it opened in late 2012. Began as casual trousers for clients, now also accessories and knitwear — [Woolmark](https://www.woolmark.com/z.archive/archive-industry/suiting/off-the-row-anderson-sheppard/), [A Visit To The Haberdashery](https://www.apetogentleman.com/visit-haberdashery/)
- Known as a "civil" tailor: its sign said civil tailors, set against houses with military roots such as Gieves & Hawkes at No. 1. Square Mile says it was not historically a royal tailor and was known as tailor to the stars — [Footprints of London](https://footprintsoflondon.com/2015/06/the-great-tailors-of-london-anderson-and-sheppard), [Square Mile](https://squaremile.com/culture/britains-finest-craft-brands/)
- House style: gentle drape, soft rounded shoulder with minimal padding (not the "rope" shoulder of military tailoring), high armhole, very light canvas, hand-drawn lapels proportioned to the chest, pleated trousers with side tabs; the person who measures also cuts the pattern — [Woolmark](https://www.woolmark.com/z.archive/archive-industry/suiting/off-the-row-anderson-sheppard/)
- Footprints describes the "Scholte style"/English drape: generous over shoulder blades and chest, sloped unpadded shoulders, roomy upper sleeve, small high armholes; it says drape came into fashion in the late 1920s and 1930s — [Footprints of London](https://footprintsoflondon.com/2015/06/the-great-tailors-of-london-anderson-and-sheppard)
- Square Mile says the drape was inspired by Guards officers' tunics: cloth draped across the chest, close at the waist, small armhole, slanted shoulder seam; other tailors copied it but none grew to similar size — [Square Mile](https://squaremile.com/culture/britains-finest-craft-brands/)
- Clients: Fred Astaire (a regular from 1923 per Footprints), Noel Coward, George Gershwin, Richard Rodgers, Ivor Novello, Cole Porter, Laurence Olivier, Marlene Dietrich (the most famous woman client per Footprints) — [Footprints of London](https://footprintsoflondon.com/2015/06/the-great-tailors-of-london-anderson-and-sheppard)
- More clients: Gary Cooper, Cary Grant, Picasso, the Duke of Windsor, Prince of Wales (Woolmark); Tom Ford a customer from 2004, Bryan Ferry, Fran Lebowitz (Wikipedia); Square Mile says the Prince of Wales first visited in December 2012 and had been a customer 20+ years — [Woolmark](https://www.woolmark.com/z.archive/archive-industry/suiting/off-the-row-anderson-sheppard/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_%26_Sheppard), [Square Mile](https://squaremile.com/culture/britains-finest-craft-brands/)
- Holds a Royal Warrant as tailors, shown as issued by HM The King; the page gives no date — [Royal Warrant Holders Association](https://www.royalwarrant.org/company/anderson-sheppard-ltd)
- New customers needed a reference from an existing client — [Footprints of London](https://footprintsoflondon.com/2015/06/the-great-tailors-of-london-anderson-and-sheppard)

## Disagreements and myths
- Per Anderson's nationality: Footprints says Swedish; no other opened source says. Treat as one source.
- Drape date: Footprints says late 1920s-1930s; the firm's own history (cited in existing entries, not opened this run) puts Scholte's work around 1900.
- Origin of drape in Guards uniform: Square Mile says so; a story repeated also in existing entries from Wikipedia/Put This On. Label as story.
- Astaire testing garments in fitting-room mirrors is a folklore anecdote (Woolmark's own wording).
- "Anderson & Simmons" first name and Simmons selling to Sheppard: only in existing entries from Put This On; I could not open that page this run (blocked), so unverified here. Sheppard's partnership date is not in any opened source.
- Royal link: Square Mile says not historically a royal tailor, yet Woolmark lists the Duke of Windsor and Prince of Wales as clients and a Royal Warrant is held now.
- Wikipedia's page lists the Savile Row founding but nothing on founders.
