# Henry Poole & Co (maker)

## Claims
- Per the firm, James Poole, a Shropshire-born linen draper, began in London in 1806 and made military tunics with his wife Mary during the Napoleonic wars; by Waterloo (1815) the firm says he was London's leading tailor (maker's claim). — [Henry Poole heritage](https://henrypoole.com/heritage-savile-row/)
- Wikipedia gives 1806 at Brunswick Square, founder James Poole, military tailoring; Mr Porter says the firm moved to Savile Row from Brunswick Square. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Poole_%26_Co); [Mr Porter](https://www.mrporter.com/en-ca/journal/fashion/the-history-behind-the-first-tailor-on-savile-row-1054326)
- The London Museum says Poole is thought to have been the first tailor on Savile Row itself: in 1846 it turned the workshops attached to its shop at 4 Old Burlington Street into a grand entrance on the Row side (then number 32, now 15). — [London Museum](https://londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/savile-row-the-heart-of-london-tailoring)
- Mr Porter says it is generally agreed to be the first tailor on the Row, in 1846; premises are now 15 Savile Row. — [Mr Porter](https://www.mrporter.com/en-ca/journal/fashion/the-history-behind-the-first-tailor-on-savile-row-1054326)
- Henry Poole, James's son, inherited in 1846 (firm) and ran it until his death in 1876; his cousin Samuel Cundey (1823-1883) then took over, and the Cundey family has held it since (five generations per Wikipedia; Simon Cundey now runs it per the firm). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Poole_%26_Co); [Henry Poole heritage](https://henrypoole.com/heritage-savile-row/)
- Royal warrants: the firm says Napoleon III gave Henry his first in 1858 and Queen Victoria the next in 1869; the Bowes Museum exhibition page repeats both. Mr Porter says over 40 warrants in total (firm's belief). — [Henry Poole heritage](https://henrypoole.com/heritage-savile-row/); [Fashion Exhibition Making](https://fashionexhibitionmaking.arts.ac.uk/henry-poole-co-founder-of-savile-row-the-art-of-bespoke-tailoring-and-wool-cloth/); [Mr Porter](https://www.mrporter.com/en-ca/journal/fashion/the-history-behind-the-first-tailor-on-savile-row-1054326)
- Edward VII, as Prince of Wales, was a customer from the early 1860s (Mr Porter); his circle and visiting royals and American millionaires drew the firm its profile. — [Mr Porter](https://www.mrporter.com/en-ca/journal/fashion/the-history-behind-the-first-tailor-on-savile-row-1054326)
- Named clients (Mr Porter): Winston Churchill, Prince Rudolf of Austria, Prince Albert Victor, Catherine Walters, Stephen Ward, Lucius Beebe, Prince Victor Duleep Singh, Buffalo Bill. The Bowes page adds a Churchill stripe fabric. — [Mr Porter](https://www.mrporter.com/en-ca/journal/fashion/the-history-behind-the-first-tailor-on-savile-row-1054326); [Fashion Exhibition Making](https://fashionexhibitionmaking.arts.ac.uk/henry-poole-co-founder-of-savile-row-the-art-of-bespoke-tailoring-and-wool-cloth/)
- Customer order ledgers 1846 to the 1950s survive: firm says over 120 volumes; Mr Porter says 126 ledgers of 1,000+ pages, restored over about 10 years by Mark Winstanley of Wyvern Bindery. — [Henry Poole heritage](https://henrypoole.com/heritage-savile-row/); [Mr Porter](https://www.mrporter.com/en-ca/journal/fashion/the-history-behind-the-first-tailor-on-savile-row-1054326)
- The firm operated away from Savile Row from 1961 to 1982 (Mr Porter). — [Mr Porter](https://www.mrporter.com/en-ca/journal/fashion/the-history-behind-the-first-tailor-on-savile-row-1054326)
- It supplies court dress to the Lord Chamberlain's office and has a livery department. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Poole_%26_Co)
- The firm says it made the first tuxedo/dinner suit for the Prince of Wales in 1865 (maker's claim). Wikipedia says 1860 and calls the firm "widely believed" to have designed the first modern dinner jacket, in the 1860s. — [Henry Poole heritage](https://henrypoole.com/heritage-savile-row/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Poole_%26_Co)
- In 1886 the Prince hosted James Potter of Tuxedo Park, New York, at Sandringham; Potter had a jacket made at Poole, wore it at the Tuxedo Club, members copied it, and it became the "tuxedo" in the US. History Today (undated page, no author) tells the same story naming "James Potter Brown", saying the Prince recommended Poole, the jacket was worn at the club's Autumn Ball, and the Delmonico's dinner remark. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Poole_%26_Co); [History Today](https://historytoday.com/node/54864)
- Frederick Scholte's Savile Row shop overlooked Henry Poole & Co. — [Wikipedia: Frederick Scholte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Scholte)

## Disagreements and myths
- Dinner jacket year: 1865 (firm, Bowes page) vs 1860 (Wikipedia). It is "widely believed", a house claim; no independent document seen in this run.
- Potter's name: James Potter (Wikipedia) vs James Potter Brown (History Today). The Potter/Tuxedo Club link is a story; Edward VII's own entry notes Favourbrook is cautious.
- Premises number: No. 32 then, 15 now (London Museum); other sources in the existing Savile Row notes say 37. Avoid a number for the early period.
- "First on the Row": Mr Porter says generally agreed; London Museum says "thought to be"; the Row's own history has earlier claims (see research/savile-row.md).
- Founding: firm says James Poole began 1806; Wikipedia says Brunswick Square; London Museum names 4 Old Burlington Street as the shop in 1846. Sequence of addresses not fully checked.
- Ledger count: over 120 (firm) vs 126 (Mr Porter).
- The firm says Henry inherited in 1846; Wikipedia says the move to the Row followed James's death. No independent death date found for James.
