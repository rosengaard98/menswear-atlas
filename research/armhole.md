# Armhole (detail)

Working name: Armhole (the small, high armhole in tailored jackets). Tailors also say armscye or scye. The Parisian Gentleman article separates the armscye (the inner opening the arm passes through) from the sleeve head (the outer top of the shoulder).

## Claims
- A high, narrow armhole lets the arms move without the chest, shoulders or padding of the jacket shifting; a low one leaves spare room under the arm and makes the body ride up when the arms are raised or held forward. — [The Armscye Height Secret](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/the-armscye-height-secret-a-crucial-detail)
- Hugo Jacomet (13 July 2015) says armscye height matters in any jacket at any price and varies widely between makers, so a buyer should check it in ready-to-wear too. He gives no measurements. — [The Armscye Height Secret](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/the-armscye-height-secret-a-crucial-detail)
- Same article: a high armscye makes the wearer stand upright and makes the arms look longer; the downside is that bending to lace shoes is harder. — [The Armscye Height Secret](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/the-armscye-height-secret-a-crucial-detail)
- Same article: a test is to raise the arms as if hanging a picture; a correct jacket should not move. It names Cifonelli and Camps de Luca as Paris houses known for high armscyes and says Claude Francois asked Camps de Luca for extremely high ones for the stage. The Fred Astaire remark is the author's opinion. — [The Armscye Height Secret](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/the-armscye-height-secret-a-crucial-detail)
- Colin Heywood of Anderson & Sheppard says British drape uses a small, high armhole that holds the jacket body in position while allowing free movement, and that the extra fullness goes into the sleeve head, not the armhole. He admits a high armhole can sound restrictive but says it "fits like a glove". Ben St George, The Rake, April 2017. — [The Timeless Elegance of British Drape](https://therake.com/stories/british-drape)
- Simon Crompton (Permanent Style, 9 March 2020): the armhole should be high so the arms move easily, but not so high that it pushes the cloth into folds. In a reply to a reader he adds that a high armhole needs more handwork, so few ready-to-wear makers offer it, and that it should sit as high as the body allows, so what is too high varies by customer. — [Video: How a jacket should fit](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2020/03/video-how-a-jacket-should-fit.html)
- Same video page: the sleeve pitch should be angled correctly so there are no wrinkles, and the sleeve looks cleaner when the arm moves forward or back. — [Video: How a jacket should fit](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2020/03/video-how-a-jacket-should-fit.html)
- Crompton (25 September 2024), with tailor Jennie Adamson: the small armhole is one of the most important functional aspects of a bespoke suit, the skill being to work a large sleeve into the small opening, so the sleeve is separated from the body and the arm moves without pulling the torso along. He lists three sleevehead finishes: smooth (extra at the bottom of the armhole), roped (a wad of padding on top) and Neapolitan (many small ripples). The article does not discuss scye depth or pitch. — [The work in a bespoke jacket: Part 3 (sleeves)](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2024/09/the-work-in-a-bespoke-jacket-part-3-sleeves.html)
- Anderson & Sheppard two-button jacket (Crompton, 25 June 2018): A&S is described as the biggest house following drape cutting; the jacket has a 7 inch shoulder, a slightly backward-leaning shoulder seam, moderate padding, and a sleevehead of wadding rolled in domette with no roping. The sleeve is wide at the top, narrowing to about 11 1/2 inches. The armhole is called "small", with no measurement, and said to work with the large sleeve and drape for comfort. — [Anderson & Sheppard two-button jacket: Style Breakdown](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2018/06/anderson-sheppard-two-button-jacket-style-breakdown.html)
- Wikipedia's drape suit page says the drape cut had extra fabric in the shoulder and armscye, with discreet horizontal drapes from roomy armholes down to a raised waist, and that by the mid-1930s the armholes were made narrower and raised, broadening the shoulders against the narrow waist. No date for the first cut is given. — [Drape suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drape_suit)
- Same page, citing Hill (2011), pp. 174-175: the American version has a smooth roll of sleeve cloth stitched into the armscye, the English one has tiny puckers from stitch tucks. — [Drape suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drape_suit)
- Wikipedia's Scholte page says he took wide shoulders, roomy armholes and a narrow waist from Household Cavalry uniforms. It says nothing on later narrowing. — [Frederick Scholte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Scholte)
- Parisian Gentleman: a large sleeve head is often sewn into a narrow armscye in Neapolitan tailoring, giving a slim line with full movement. — [The Armscye Height Secret](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/the-armscye-height-secret-a-crucial-detail)
- Forum, not authoritative: Fedora Lounge posters say "high" is a point on a continuum, that the aim is the smallest armhole one can wear comfortably, that shape (the hook behind the arm) matters as much as height, and that the angle the sleeve sets in matters too. One poster reports a tailor's pattern armhole of 24 1/2 inches against a body measurement of 20 inches; another recalls about 20 inches for Indy Magnoli. Unverified. — [Coat arm holes: how high is high](https://www.thefedoralounge.com/threads/coat-arm-holes-how-high-is-high.41619)
- Promotional: a Hoi An custom tailor (Nathan Tailors, 7 September 2026) says a larger size usually means a deeper armhole, so moving up a size can worsen lifting, and defines pitch as the sleeve's rotation inside the armhole. Treat as a seller's claim. — [Why does your suit pull when you raise your arms](https://www.nathantailors.com/en/blog/why-your-suit-pulls-when-you-raise-your-arms)

## Disagreements and myths
- Roomy or small? Wikipedia says Scholte's early drape armholes were roomy and later raised and narrowed (mid-1930s); Heywood speaks only of today's house practice, a high armhole. Together they are consistent but no source says who narrowed them. The Scholte page and the drape page both say the roomy armhole came from the Household Cavalry; this is a story, not documented.
- "Bigger armhole, more freedom" is a common belief (a Permanent Style commenter reports a tailor saying so, then finding the opposite). Crompton, Heywood and Jacomet all say the reverse. Crompton's own caveat: too high pushes the cloth into folds.
- How high is high has no standard number in anything I opened; forum figures are unverified.
- Jacomet's claims about Astaire and Francois are anecdote.

## Construction notes (garments and details)
- Armhole = the opening the sleeve is sewn into; the cut edge runs from the shoulder point round the underarm, front and back pieces meeting at the side seam. The "bottom" of the armhole (underarm point) is where height is judged: high means close to the armpit, little spare cloth beneath it.
- High and small: the body of the jacket stays still when the arm lifts. Low and large: more of the body goes up with the sleeve. Crompton's limit: not so high it makes the cloth fold.
- A small armhole needs a sleeve cut larger than the opening; the excess is eased into the sleeve head, either smooth (excess at the bottom of the armhole), roped, or rippled (Neapolitan). A&S uses a wadding roll in domette, no roping, a sleeve about 11 1/2 inches at its narrowest in the comparison.
- Sleeve pitch: the rotation of the sleeve in the armhole; wrong pitch gives diagonal wrinkles. Not covered in depth by any source I opened.
- Shape counts as well as height (forum: the hook behind the arm, the egg shape).
- For the illustrator: the armhole is drawn at the inside of the shoulder, from the shoulder point curving down to a point high under the arm, close to the chest. A crop of the existing drape-cut or spalla-camicia front view is the sensible picture; the drape-cut hotspot at 431,232 already marks it.
