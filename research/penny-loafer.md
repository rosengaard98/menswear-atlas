# Penny loafer (garment, shoe)

## Claims
- Nils Gregoriusson Tveranger (1874-1953), a shoemaker from Aurland in Sogn, Norway, is credited with the Aurland shoe. Dates vary by source: first design c. 1908 with a 1910 Bergen exhibition diploma (Wikipedia, Gentleman's Gazette); "Aurlandskoen" created in 1930 (Scan Magazine); patented "as early as 1920" (Port). Treat all as unverified secondary claims. — [Slip-on shoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slip-on_shoe); [How a Norwegian Fisherman Invented the Penny Loafer](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/penny-loafer-history/); [Aurland Skofabrikk](https://scanmagazine.co.uk/aurland-skofabrikk-the-original-penny-loafer/); [Port](https://www.port-magazine.com/fashion/an-brief-history-of-the-loafer/)
- Tveranger learned shoemaking in America (Analog Shift: left for Boston 1891, back 1894; Gentleman's Gazette: went as a boy, back c. 1894) and combined American/Iroquois moccasin ideas with the local slip-on "tese"/"teser". — [Analog Shift](https://www.analogshift.com/blogs/transmissions/penny-loafer-shoe-history); [Scan Magazine](https://scanmagazine.co.uk/aurland-skofabrikk-the-original-penny-loafer/)
- English sportsmen visiting Norway (fishing) are said to have noticed and carried home the local slip-ons; Scan says British lords on fishing holidays had shoes repaired in Aurland from the 1880s. — [Port](https://www.port-magazine.com/fashion/an-brief-history-of-the-loafer/); [Scan Magazine](https://scanmagazine.co.uk/aurland-skofabrikk-the-original-penny-loafer/)
- Exported shoes were called "Norwegian moccasins" in the US. Aurland-area factories made about 100,000 pairs in 1950, many exported to America (Scan). — [Slip-on shoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slip-on_shoe); [Scan Magazine](https://scanmagazine.co.uk/aurland-skofabrikk-the-original-penny-loafer/)
- Esquire route (Analog Shift, Port): a staffer saw the shoe in Palm Beach; editor Arnold Gingrich looked for a US maker, via Rogers Peet and then G.H. Bass. No year given by either; Port places it mid-1930s. — [Analog Shift](https://www.analogshift.com/blogs/transmissions/penny-loafer-shoe-history); [Port](https://www.port-magazine.com/fashion/an-brief-history-of-the-loafer/)
- G.H. Bass & Co. of Wilton, Maine (founded 1876; making moccasins by 1906 per Gentleman's Gazette review) launched the Weejun in 1936; first advertised in the New York Herald Tribune on 27 May 1936 at $6.50 (Analog Shift, Port). Name chosen to sound like "Norwegian". Wikipedia gives 1934 in text and 1936 in its table. — [Analog Shift](https://www.analogshift.com/blogs/transmissions/penny-loafer-shoe-history); [Port](https://www.port-magazine.com/fashion/an-brief-history-of-the-loafer/); [Gentleman's Gazette review](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/weejuns-penny-loafers-review/); [Slip-on shoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slip-on_shoe)
- By the 1950s the Weejun was popular with US prep-school and college students; "penny loafer" name arose on campuses, mid-century. Analog Shift says Ivy League types adopted it. Port says nobody knows exactly why the trend started. — [Analog Shift](https://www.analogshift.com/blogs/transmissions/penny-loafer-shoe-history); [Gentleman's Gazette review](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/weejuns-penny-loafers-review/); [Port](https://www.port-magazine.com/fashion/an-brief-history-of-the-loafer/)
- Later wearers named by Analog Shift (1960s): Miles Davis, Elvis, Cary Grant, Steve McQueen, James Dean, Paul Newman, JFK; Wall Street in the 1980s. Wikipedia: peaks in the mid-to-late 1960s and early 1980s to early 1990s. Single-source, not checked elsewhere. — [Analog Shift](https://www.analogshift.com/blogs/transmissions/penny-loafer-shoe-history); [Slip-on shoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slip-on_shoe)
- Bass ownership: sons John and Willard from 1925; Chesebrough-Ponds 1978; Phillips-Van Heusen 1987; G3 from 2013 (single source). — [Gentleman's Gazette review](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/weejuns-penny-loafers-review/)
- Wildsmith (London) is credited by Wikipedia with a 1926 house slip-on for George VI (called the "first loafer"); the article's Spalding claim is flagged there as uncited. Not found in other sources opened. — [Slip-on shoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slip-on_shoe)

## Disagreements and myths
- Penny-in-the-slot: the pay-phone story (Royal Mint page, no source given) is doubtful, since Bell pay phones took dimes (Analog Shift) and a call cost more than a cent (Gentleman's Gazette). The Gazette review says early students kept dimes in the cut-out and pennies became fashionable later. Gentleman's Gazette suggests copper accent as likelier; this is its inference. Scan Magazine says explanations for the pocket vary: fashion detail or a symbol of resistance in WWII. Label all as stories. — [Analog Shift](https://www.analogshift.com/blogs/transmissions/penny-loafer-shoe-history); [Gentleman's Gazette](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/penny-loafer-history/); [Royal Mint](https://www.royalmint.com/stories/collect/the-history-of-the-penny-loafer/); [Scan Magazine](https://scanmagazine.co.uk/aurland-skofabrikk-the-original-penny-loafer/)
- Weejun name "short for Norwegians" is repeated by Scan, Wikipedia, Gentleman's Gazette review; it is the maker's naming, intent only inferred.
- Dates for the Aurland shoe (1908/1920/1930) and Weejun (1934/1936) disagree. 1936 has the Herald Tribune ad date from two sources and Bass's own page also refers to 1936. The Bass page lists no Norway origin. Aurland's 1930 date comes from the factory-related Scan piece, so a maker's claim.
- Royal Mint page places Aurland "just west of Norway", which is an error; ignore it.
- The Spalding (New Hampshire) claim appears only on Wikipedia and is flagged uncited. Do not repeat it.
- Wildsmith 1926 "first loafer" is Wikipedia only.

## Construction notes (garments and details)
- Slip-on, laceless, low-cut shoe with the toe made as a moccasin: a U-shaped plug/apron stitched to the vamp. G.H. Bass says the Weejun upper is cut in four parts for the tubular moccasin and hand-stitched; the hand stitching forms the design. — [G.H. Bass](https://www.ghbass.com/pages/how-we-make-our-shoes) (maker's page; does not use "apron")
- Moccasin-like seam across the top of the vamp (Analog Shift: "moccasin-like welt across the top"), separate low-heeled sole, exposed ankle. — [Analog Shift](https://www.analogshift.com/blogs/transmissions/penny-loafer-shoe-history)
- Saddle: a leather strap across the instep, not always present on loafers. Original 1936 saddle had a diamond-shaped cut-out (the "penny slot"). Modern Larson has beefrolls on the saddle strap; Logan has a flat strap. — [Gentleman's Gazette review](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/weejuns-penny-loafers-review/)
- Beefroll: made with two needles, gathering vamp edge leather into a crimped roll (Bass). — [G.H. Bass](https://www.ghbass.com/pages/how-we-make-our-shoes)
- Outsole: leather, stitched through the upper, edge trimmed and stained; rubber top lifts on heels in the reviewed pairs; handcrafted welt on the review pairs. Leather footbed over foam on current Bass. — [G.H. Bass](https://www.ghbass.com/pages/how-we-make-our-shoes); [Gentleman's Gazette review](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/weejuns-penny-loafers-review/)
- Aurland shoe: self-bearing, form-fitting, made from cowhide; leather soaked and stretched to shape. — [Scan Magazine](https://scanmagazine.co.uk/aurland-skofabrikk-the-original-penny-loafer/)
- For the flat: top view or side view, apron with seam following the toe in a U, saddle strap across the instep with a diamond or slot cut-out, low stacked heel, heel counter. Proportions are not given in any source; no measurements found.
