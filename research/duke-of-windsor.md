# Duke of Windsor, Edward VIII (person)

## Claims
- Born 23 June 1894 at White Lodge, Richmond Park; Prince of Wales from 23 June 1910; king 20 January to 11 December 1936; styled Duke of Windsor after abdicating; died in Paris 28 May 1972. — [Edward VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_VIII)
- Wikipedia says his fashion sense was a hallmark of the era and that he set men's fashion at the height of his popularity. — [Edward VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_VIII)
- During his 1924 visit to the US, *Men's Wear* observed that the average young American man cared more about the Prince's clothes (as Wikipedia quotes it, citing Alan Flusser, *Dressing the Man*, 2002, p. 8). Second-hand: I did not open Flusser or *Men's Wear*. — [Edward VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_VIII)
- Frederick Scholte (born Amsterdam 1865, died 1948) was the Prince's principal tailor from 1919 to his death in 1948; Wikipedia says some sources wrongly give 1959. — [Frederick Scholte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Scholte)
- Scholte is credited with inventing the "drape" suit for the Prince, introduced in the 1930s, borrowing wide shoulders, roomy armholes and a narrow waist from Household Cavalry uniforms; Wikipedia calls it a revolution against the tighter Victorian cut. Wikipedia's own sources include Anderson & Sheppard's company history and *The Rake* (2017); I did not open those. — [Frederick Scholte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Scholte)
- The Met says the Prince introduced midnight blue evening wear in the 1920s as an alternative to black, to soften formal dress and build a fashion-leader image in the press. — [Evening suit, Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/82170)
- Met evening suit 1974.185.3a-c: designed by F. Scholte; H. Harris, a New York tailor trained in London, made duplicate trousers for the Duke when he was Governor of the Bahamas in WWII, which the Duke called an "international compromise" (trousers New York, jackets London). Given by the Duchess of Windsor in 1974. — [Evening suit, Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/82170)
- V&A holds a 1940 single-breasted lounge suit in mustard wool Glen Urquhart / Prince of Wales check, by Trimingham (London or Bermuda), worn by the Duke, who gave it to Cecil Beaton for his 1971 exhibition *Fashion: An Anthology*. V&A calls him a leader of men's fashion known for casual style and bold colours, patterns and textures. T.717&A-1974. — [V&A O16804](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O16804)
- Permanent Style says he favoured checks more than his contemporaries, which helped popularise a glen plaid variant, and wore spectator shoes and suede shoes that his father George V disliked (the father called them footwear of a "bounder and a cad"). The post has an internal error (names Edward VII as his father) and no dates, so treat as weak. — [Permanent Style, 2010](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2010/03/be-a-rogue-like-the-big-bad-wolf.html)
- Fair Isle: V&A Dundee blog says a Fair Isle jumper design from the 1920s was famously worn by Princes of Wales while golfing and in a painting, knitted in the original by Maggie Simpson; it boosted Shetland and Fair Isle knitters. No date for when he wore it. The blog says "Princes of Wales" and its caption names Edward, so the attribution is loose. — [Mary Williamson, V&A Dundee](https://www.vam.ac.uk/blog/dundee/mary-williamson-championing-scottish-tradition)
- Windsor knot: the Duke said he neither invented nor tied it; he tied a simple four-in-hand and got fullness from broad, thickly interlined ties made with a tie maker in the 1920s. He called the Windsor a double knot in a narrow tie. Source relies only on his book *A Family Album*, unopened. — [Windsor: The Knot the Duke Never Tied](https://academia-cravatica.hr/?p=36515)
- 1922 press photograph (Royal Collection RCIN 2000449) shows him as St Andrews Golf Captain driving off a tee in plus fours. — [Royal Collection](https://col.rct.uk/collection/2000449/edward-viii-king-of-great-britain-1894-1972-when-prince-of-wales-at-st-andrews)

## Disagreements and myths
- Windsor knot: the name is attached to him, but he said he did not invent or tie it (his own account, via academia-cravatica).
- Scholte end date of service: 1948 per Wikipedia, which says some sources give 1959.
- Drape: credited to Scholte and the Prince; the claim comes from Wikipedia and its cited trade sources, not independently checked. Expect the drape's origin to be disputed elsewhere (e.g. Anderson & Sheppard's own claims); not verified here.
- Fair Isle: no source opened dates the Prince's wearing; the often-repeated 1921-22 St Andrews story was NOT confirmed. The Royal Collection photo of 1922 shows plus fours, no mention of Fair Isle; the NZ newspaper (Manawatu Herald, 3 Oct 1922, "Prince on Golf Links") covers the St Andrews round but says nothing of a jumper.
