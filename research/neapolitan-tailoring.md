# Neapolitan tailoring (style)

## Claims
- Neapolitan tailoring is generally dated to the early 1900s in Naples, as a softer answer to structured British tailoring; its roots are said to reach back to the late 19th century. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_tailoring); [Parisian Gentleman, Italian tailor](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/the-italian-tailor-naples-and-the-neapolitan-jacket)
- Before the new style, local suiting looked like Savile Row or Roman work; Salvatore Morziello's Naples shop sold Anglophile goods (Lock & Co., Floris, Swaine Adeney Brigg). — [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/the-history-and-anatomy-of-neapolitan-tailoring)
- Gennaro "Bebè" Rubinacci ran a house called London House; he admired English cloth and style. In the 1930s he is said to have proposed a softer, unlined version of the drape cut of Frederick Scholte and Anderson & Sheppard. — [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/the-history-and-anatomy-of-neapolitan-tailoring)
- Vincenzo Attolini, apprentice of Morziello and head cutter at Rubinacci, is credited with the original Neapolitan jacket: no pads, little or no lining, very light. Parisian Gentleman says he could make a jacket that "folded up to eight times". — [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/the-history-and-anatomy-of-neapolitan-tailoring); [Parisian Gentleman, Italian tailor](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/the-italian-tailor-naples-and-the-neapolitan-jacket); [Parisian Gentleman, Stile Latino](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/stile-latino-by-vincenzo-attolini)
- Wikipedia instead dates the shaping of a distinct light jacket to the 1920s, naming Antonio Caggiula and Vincenzo Attolini (its citation for this looks weak). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_tailoring)
- Angelo Blasi, a contemporary of Attolini, is described as taking English structured coats as his model. — [Parisian Gentleman, Italian tailor](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/the-italian-tailor-naples-and-the-neapolitan-jacket)
- Cesare Attolini (Vincenzo's son) devised in the 1960s the "Neapolitan System", in which each craftsman specialises in one task; he had worked for Enrico Isaia, whose house adopted it. — [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/the-history-and-anatomy-of-neapolitan-tailoring)
- Ciro Paone founded Kiton in 1968. Other houses named: Antonio Panico (22 years at Rubinacci, then own workshop), Orazio Luciano (founded 1992, ex-Kiton and Isaia), Solito, Caliendo, Dalcuore (1966). — [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/the-history-and-anatomy-of-neapolitan-tailoring)
- Wikipedia says Fausto Sarli and Mario Valentino helped the style's international reach in the 1950s via Giorgini's Florence shows and a 1954 "Congress of Fashion" in Naples (secondary, citation Motta 2018). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_tailoring)
- Output figures per The Rake: Attolini about 11,000 suits a year at about 30 man-hours each; Kiton about 20,000 jackets; a bespoke Rubinacci suit 54 man-hours. — [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/the-history-and-anatomy-of-neapolitan-tailoring)
- Today Rubinacci offers three shoulders: spalla camicia, spalla Milanese (more padding, light body) and spalla Inglese (strongly padded, firmer canvas). Luca Rubinacci calls the house's jackets somewhat more English than typical Neapolitan: a little longer, lower buttoning point. Tailors have moved to a new facility near Attolini (35 tailors). — [Permanent Style, Sept 2026](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2026/09/the-varied-tailoring-of-rubinacci-today.html)
- Early Neapolitan tailors often chalked directly onto cloth from notebook notes; Rubinacci moved to cloth patterns under Mariano Rubinacci. — [Permanent Style](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2026/09/the-varied-tailoring-of-rubinacci-today.html)

## Disagreements and myths
- Origin: the Rubinacci and Attolini families are reported to feud over who made the Neapolitan jacket; Parisian Gentleman's author gives both credit. — [Parisian Gentleman, Stile Latino](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/stile-latino-by-vincenzo-attolini)
- Decade: early 1900s (Parisian Gentleman, Wikipedia), 1920s (Wikipedia on Caggiula/Attolini), 1930s for Rubinacci's unlined drape (The Rake).
- Caraceni: Parisian Gentleman's Stile Latino piece says he came first and influenced Attolini; the other Parisian Gentleman piece says "we'll never know for sure". Caraceni's name is claimed by several houses. The Stile Latino article is brand-sponsored editorial.
- Not every Naples suit is Neapolitan: structured Roman-style suits are made there too. — [Parisian Gentleman, Italian tailor](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/the-italian-tailor-naples-and-the-neapolitan-jacket)
- Features such as 3-roll-2 and high gorge also occur in Paris and London tailoring (same source).
- Unverified: "fewer fittings", "folds eight times" (maker/anecdote).

## Construction notes (garments and details)
- Spalla camicia: shoulder sewn like a shirt sleeve, no pad; sleevehead cut much larger than the armhole (Wikipedia says about 10 cm, citing Studio 104) and hand-set into a high, snug armhole, giving shirring (grinze, repecchia). Con rollino adds a small roll of wadding to hide it. Variants: spalla cadente (natural), spalla insellata (saddle). — [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/the-history-and-anatomy-of-neapolitan-tailoring); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_tailoring)
- Lining minimal or none, piped inner seams; three-roll-two buttoning; high gorge; boat-shaped barchetta chest pocket; curved patch pockets (pignata); double hand backstitch (doppia impuntura) on lapels and pockets; front dart running to the hem; moderate waist suppression; slightly short body; tapered sleeves. — [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/the-history-and-anatomy-of-neapolitan-tailoring); [Parisian Gentleman, Italian tailor](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/the-italian-tailor-naples-and-the-neapolitan-jacket)
- Unsourced on Wikipedia: lapels up to 4 in, side vents up to 12 in (do not use without another source).
