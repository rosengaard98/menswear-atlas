# Loden coat (garment)

Regions: germany (Bavaria/Germany is a main weaving and retail base: Lodenfrey Munich, Mehler) plus other (Austria, South Tyrol). Sources tie the coat to Austria and Germany together (Permanent Style, Austria-Forum), so `regions: [germany, other]` is supportable. Framing: the coat and the cloth are inseparable; the entry can stay `loden-coat`, with the cloth covered in the body (a separate `loden` detail entry is possible later).

## Claims
- Loden is a woven, traditionally woollen cloth that is fulled (soaked in hot water and soap, then pounded or stamped), not felted; fulling densifies the surface, hides the weave and keeps out wind and water. Roetzel says calling it felt is wrong. — [Loden: The cloth, the coat, the history](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2025/03/loden-the-cloth-the-coat-the-history.html)
- Alpine fulling mills have existed since about the 15th century; peasants spun their sheep's wool, travelling weavers wove it, and the cloth went to a local mill. — [Permanent Style](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2025/03/loden-the-cloth-the-coat-the-history.html)
- Britannica says peasants handwove the cloth in the 16th century and describes the coat as of Tyrolean origin, thick, soft and waterproof, most commonly bluish green, sometimes with a stand-up collar and braid or silver buttons. (Date differs from the 15th-century mill claim; see Disagreements.) — [Britannica, loden coat](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/345951)
- The word comes from Old High German "lodo", rough cloth. — [Wikipedia, Loden cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loden_cape); [Weinstrasse](https://www.weinstrasse.com/en/leisure-activities/markets-fairs-and-shopping/products-made-in-south-tyrol/the-south-tyrolean-loden/)
- Original cloth was coarse mountain-sheep wool; merino, cashmere or angora were added in the early 20th century for softer, lighter versions; some now contain a little synthetic. — [Permanent Style](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2025/03/loden-the-cloth-the-coat-the-history.html)
- Original colours grey, green and brown; Schladminger grey is best known; green is the international association, largely through the Hubertus coat. Green loden was long worn by huntsmen and foresters in Germany and Austria; the cloth is quiet, which suits hunting. — [Permanent Style](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2025/03/loden-the-cloth-the-coat-the-history.html)
- In the mid-19th century production grew in scale, and Austrian royalty popularised it as country wear, spreading to Germany (Roetzel compares this to Victoria and tweed). Under Emperor Franz Josef it became established among noble families. — [Permanent Style](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2025/03/loden-the-cloth-the-coat-the-history.html); [Weinstrasse](https://www.weinstrasse.com/en/leisure-activities/markets-fairs-and-shopping/products-made-in-south-tyrol/the-south-tyrolean-loden/)
- Lodenfrey of Munich says (as reported by Roetzel) it wove a truly water-repellent loden in 1872, "Strichloden", finished with thistles (teasels), today about 80% wool, 20% alpaca. A maker's claim. — [Permanent Style](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2025/03/loden-the-cloth-the-coat-the-history.html)
- Finishing: brushing with fuller's teasel and clipping the nap, repeated several times; loden is classed as a kind of melton. — [Wikipedia, Loden cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loden_cape)
- Austrian lexicon: small fulling mills mainly in Styria and Tyrol; the trade became export-oriented and made hats, jackets and coats including the Hubertus coat. — [Austria-Forum, AEIOU Loden](https://austria-forum.org/af/AEIOU/Loden/Loden_english?skin=raw)
- The Hubertus coat (named for the hunters' patron saint, implied but not explained in the sources; do not state) is the classic form. Wearers: Duke of Edinburgh often; Duke of Kent; Lady Diana Spencer wore a green one over evening dress to the Ritz in 1980. Popular with classically dressed men in Italy, Spain, France, Switzerland. — [Permanent Style](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2025/03/loden-the-cloth-the-coat-the-history.html)
- Named current makers: Schneider's of Salzburg (bankrupt 2023), Plankl (Vienna), Eduard Meier (Munich), Lodenfrey; British: Cordings, Chrysalis, Private White VC. Weavers: Leichtfried, Steiner, Lodenwalker, Moessmer, Mehler, Kreutner. — [Permanent Style](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2025/03/loden-the-cloth-the-coat-the-history.html)
- The Loden Museum in Vandoies (South Tyrol) covers origin and production. — [Weinstrasse](https://www.weinstrasse.com/en/leisure-activities/markets-fairs-and-shopping/products-made-in-south-tyrol/the-south-tyrolean-loden/)

## Disagreements and myths
- Felted vs fulled: Cordings' site calls it felted; Roetzel says it is fulled woven cloth. Wikipedia's text says "felt-like".
- Dates: Britannica 16th-century handweaving; Permanent Style 15th-century fulling mills; Weinstrasse and Austria-Forum give no dates. Say "by the 15th or 16th century" and attribute.
- Britannica places origin in "Loderers", Austria; Wikipedia and Weinstrasse say Tyrol/South Tyrol. Britannica's place name was not confirmed elsewhere; avoid.
- No single "original" Hubertus design exists (Roetzel).
- Reputation: Roetzel says loden looks old-fashioned through styling, not the cloth; a reader comment calls it rural and conservative in Austria/Bavaria (comment, weak).
- Roetzel says Schneider's moved production to central Europe; reader comments disagree about its successor. Skip.

## Construction notes (garments and details)
- Hubertus coat: deep inverted pleat at back (originally for shooting), underarm vents, leather "football" buttons, pockets with inside openings reaching trouser or jacket pockets. Shoulders, chest and upper back often double-layered for rain; some makers use raglan or regular sleeveheads. Traditionally unlined (lining wears faster than cloth; double-faced loden sometimes used). Hubertus collar (Wetterfleck cape shares it). Stand-up collar with braid is the Britannica variant. Length not stated in sources; illustrator should choose roughly knee length.
- Related garments: Loden Stutzer (shorter coat); Lodenkotze / Wetterfleck (cape with arm vents, two long panels, worn over a backpack). — [Permanent Style](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2025/03/loden-the-cloth-the-coat-the-history.html)
- Colour: green, grey, brown. Short pile, dense surface.
