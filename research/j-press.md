# J. Press (maker)

## Claims
- J. Press is dated to 1902 and to Jacobi Press, a Jewish immigrant tailor who set up in New Haven to serve Yale men. Dates are the maker's and family's own. — [J. Press, our history](https://jpress.com/pages/our-history-1); [Wikipedia: J. Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Press)
- Richard Press (grandson) says Jacobi learned tailoring and English as an intern at J.C. Goldbaum's Chapel Street shop, bought that business in 1902 (Goldbaum & Press, later J. Press) and in 1907 bought the building at 262 York Street, opposite Pierson College. — [Golden Years: Goodbye To All That](https://www.ivy-style.com/?p=5420), Ivy Style, 19 Dec 2013
- A New York store opened in 1912, placed between the Yale and Harvard Clubs (Wikipedia cites GQ, 2014 for this). A Cambridge, Mass. store ran 86 years and closed in August 2018. — [Wikipedia: J. Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Press)
- Wikipedia lists Duke Ellington, Cary Grant and Robert Frost as early customers; Ivy Style's J. Press-related page lists Cole Porter, Frank Sinatra, Miles Davis, Averell Harriman, Robert Frost, George Plimpton and several presidents. Treat client lists as house marketing. — [Wikipedia: J. Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Press); [J. Press, our history](https://jpress.com/pages/our-history-1)
- Irving E. Press (Jacobi's son) led the firm in its heyday; the Shaggy Dog Shetland sweater is credited to that era. — [Wikipedia: J. Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Press)
- Signature goods: Shaggy Dog sweaters, Indian madras jackets, Oxford button-down shirts, repp ties, three-button sack jackets, plain-front trousers. — [Wikipedia: J. Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Press)
- Jackets are described as sack or straight-fall, unpadded shoulders, three buttons with the top left undone; regimental ties about 3 1/4 in. — [New Haven Independent, J. Press marks 120 years](https://www.newhavenindependent.org/article/j_press_marks_120_years_with_a_new_store)
- A Cambridge store manager (2009) said J. Press was long known for the closest thing to a natural-shouldered garment in the industry. This is one employee's claim. — [Tradition and Change: The J. Press Interview, Part Two](https://www.ivy-style.com/?p=634), Christian Chensvold, Ivy Style, 15 Jul 2009
- Japan: J. Press licensed its name in Japan in 1974 (said to be the first American brand so licensed); Onward Kashiyama, the licensee, bought the company in 1986, ending family control. Japanese business is reported as about six times the American one. — [Wikipedia: J. Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Press) (cites DNR 2007, NYT 1986 and 2009; I did not open those)
- Maker's page says J. Press is widely credited with creating the Ivy League look, and was prominent in the 1965 book Take Ivy and in Tokyo's Ivy boom. Maker's claim. — [J. Press, our history](https://jpress.com/pages/our-history-1)
- J. Press ran a pop-up in Isetan Shinjuku during the October-November 2013 Ivy Style exhibition in Tokyo (MFIT show). — [Ivy Style in Tokyo](https://fitnyc.edu/museum/news/archive/2014/ivy-style-in-tokyo.php), Patricia Mears, The Museum at FIT
- The New Haven store was badly damaged in the February 2013 storm (Wikipedia names Nemo); a new store at 262 Elm St. opened around the 120th anniversary (c. 2022). Owner is Japanese; Richard Press, 84, was consultant and company historian at that time. — [New Haven Independent](https://www.newhavenindependent.org/article/j_press_marks_120_years_with_a_new_store); [Wikipedia: J. Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Press)
- Jack Carlson named creative director in 2025 (Wikipedia, citing FT). — [Wikipedia: J. Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Press)

## Disagreements and myths
- Origin: Wikipedia and the maker say Jacobi came from Latvia (Wikipedia: intended to be a rabbi, worked for an uncle in Middletown, CT, then founded J. Press in 1902). Richard Press says he came from the "Pale of Russia" and bought the existing Goldbaum shop in 1902, i.e. not a from-scratch start. NHI says he sold door to door to Yale students. Not reconciled.
- Building: Wikipedia says built 1863 as a grocer's house; Richard Press says built c. 1860 and bought by Jacobi in 1907. Minor.
- "Created the Ivy League look" is the maker's claim; Brooks Brothers has an equal or earlier claim to the sack suit.
- Ownership: NHI says "Japanese parent"; Wikipedia gives the 1986 Onward Kashiyama purchase. Not in conflict.
- The Fitzgerald "J. Pressed tweed" letter (1936) is on Wikipedia, citing Tablet 2012; I did not check the original.

## Construction notes (garments and details)
- Three-button sack jacket, top button left open (roll to the middle button); soft, unpadded natural shoulder, straight fall, no darts implied by the sources; plain-front trousers with a 1 3/4 in cuff (Wikipedia, as the company's advice); regimental repp tie c. 3 1/4 in; Oxford button-down; Shaggy Dog Shetland sweater; madras jackets; Chesterfield overcoats with velvet collar. Mostly source-level descriptions; no stitching measurements found.
