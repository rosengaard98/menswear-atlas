# Ralph Lauren (person)

Living person (born 1939). No photograph as the picture. The company (Polo Ralph Lauren, later Ralph Lauren Corporation) is best covered inside this entry.

## Claims
- Born Ralph Lifshitz in the Bronx on 14 October 1939; he and his brother Jerry changed the family surname to Lauren as teenagers (Biography.com and EBSCO say sixteen; Social Life says 1955, which would make him fifteen or sixteen). — [Biography.com](https://www.biography.com/history-culture/ralph-lauren), [EBSCO](https://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/ralph-lauren), [Social Life](https://sociallifemagazine.com/the-archive/ralph-lauren-hamptons-montauk/)
- Studied business at Baruch College, served in the Army (1962-64 per Social Life), then sold clothing at Brooks Brothers before moving into neckwear (Rivetz, then selling for the tie maker Beau Brummell). — [Biography.com](https://www.biography.com/history-culture/ralph-lauren), [Social Life](https://sociallifemagazine.com/the-archive/ralph-lauren-hamptons-montauk/)
- 1967: he began selling his own wide ties under the name "Polo", made by Beau Brummell. EBSCO gives the width as about four inches against roughly two and a half for early-1960s ties. First-year sales about $500,000. — [EBSCO](https://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/ralph-lauren), [Gentleman's Gazette](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/ralph-lauren-gentleman-of-style/), [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/764668) (confirms 1967 ties under Polo name)
- Bloomingdale's first wanted only a store-label version (Social Life: "Sutton East") or narrower ties (EBSCO), then took the ties on his terms; the company says Bloomingdale's opened its first single-designer boutique for him in 1970. — [Social Life](https://sociallifemagazine.com/the-archive/ralph-lauren-hamptons-montauk/), [EBSCO](https://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/ralph-lauren), [Ralph Lauren 50th timeline](https://www.ralphlauren.ca/rl-50th-anniversary-the-timeline)
- 1968: first full menswear collection. 1970: Coty menswear award (Biography.com). 1971: first freestanding store (Rodeo Drive, company's claim: first by an American designer) and a women's shirt line carrying the embroidered pony. — [Ralph Lauren 50th timeline](https://www.ralphlauren.ca/rl-50th-anniversary-the-timeline), [Biography.com](https://www.biography.com/history-culture/ralph-lauren)
- 1972: short-sleeve mesh/cotton polo shirt in 24 colours with the polo-player logo, which became the signature item. — [Biography.com](https://www.biography.com/history-culture/ralph-lauren), [EBSCO](https://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/ralph-lauren), [Ralph Lauren 50th timeline](https://www.ralphlauren.ca/rl-50th-anniversary-the-timeline)
- 1974: *The Great Gatsby*: the period clothes came from his Polo collection, except Redford's pink suit, which he designed (company and Gentleman's Gazette agree). 1977: *Annie Hall*; Keaton's look used pieces of his, and he says it reflected her own style. — [Ralph Lauren 50th timeline](https://www.ralphlauren.ca/rl-50th-anniversary-the-timeline), [Gentleman's Gazette](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/ralph-lauren-gentleman-of-style/), [Social Life](https://sociallifemagazine.com/the-archive/ralph-lauren-hamptons-montauk/)
- 1976: Coty Hall of Fame for menswear. Late 1970s onward: fragrance (1978), Western wear (1978), Home (1983), per Gentleman's Gazette. — [Gentleman's Gazette](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/ralph-lauren-gentleman-of-style/)
- 21 April 1986: flagship opened in the Rhinelander mansion, Madison Avenue at 72nd Street (built 1894-98, architect Alexander Mackintosh of Kimball & Thompson; NYC landmark 1976). — [Wikipedia: Gertrude Rhinelander Waldo House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gertrude_Rhinelander_Waldo_House); Biography.com agrees on 1986 and the mansion
- 1994/95: Purple Label, an English-leaning tailoring line (Gentleman's Gazette Purple Label review says 1994; the Gentleman's Gazette profile says 1995). Described as "an American take on Savile Row", soft unstructured tailoring; early pieces largely made in England, now mostly Italy. — [Gentleman's Gazette: Purple Label](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/ralph-lauren-purple-label-to-buy/), [Gentleman's Gazette profile](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/ralph-lauren-gentleman-of-style/)
- 11 June 1997: public listing (NYSE: RL). September 2015: stepped down as CEO, stayed executive chairman and chief creative officer. — [Biography.com](https://www.biography.com/history-culture/ralph-lauren)
- Method: Britannica describes the business as built around an image of an "elite American lifestyle"; Social Life says he watched Hollywood films (Cary Grant, Astaire etc.) as a boy. The brand sells a lifestyle expressed across clothing, home, fragrance. — [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/764668), [Social Life](https://sociallifemagazine.com/the-archive/ralph-lauren-hamptons-montauk/)
- Gentleman's Gazette says he revived 1930s shapes (wide ties, double-breasted suits) and mixed tailoring with Western workwear. — [Gentleman's Gazette](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/ralph-lauren-gentleman-of-style/)
- Context: the Museum at FIT's Ivy Style exhibition (14 Sep 2012-5 Jan 2013) says Ivy, "now often referred to as preppy", has been appropriated by many designers (names none). Brooks Brothers and J. Press adapted English pieces before WWII. — [FIT Ivy Style](https://sites.fitnyc.edu/depts/museum/Ivy_Style/)

## Disagreements and myths
- Start-up loan: Biography.com says $30,000; EBSCO says $50,000 from Norman Hilton. Unresolved.
- Name change age: sixteen (Biography.com, EBSCO) versus 1955 (Social Life).
- Womenswear: Biography.com says a women's suit line after the 1970 Coty award; the company and Gentleman's Gazette say 1971 (shirts with pony) and a full collection in 1972. Treat 1971 as the first women's shirts.
- Purple Label date: 1994 vs 1995 across two Gentleman's Gazette pages.
- Redford's wardrobe: EBSCO says he designed Redford's wardrobe for Gatsby; the company and Gentleman's Gazette say most clothes were off-the-rack Polo, with only the pink suit made for the film. Prefer the latter, noting it is partly the maker's claim.
- First-year ties sales ($500,000) is repeated across sources but originates in company lore.
- The brand's English-gentleman and Ivy images are constructed: his own story is of a Bronx childhood and Hollywood films, not an upper-class background. I could not open a critical academic source for this; do not state it as established beyond the biography facts.
