# Frederick Scholte (person)

## Claims
- Born Friedrich Petrus Scholte, 19 July 1865, Amsterdam; died 2 December 1948 at Holloway Sanatorium, Virginia Water, Surrey. Moved to London in the 1880s; naturalised 1899 (sponsored by Viscount Ridley). — [Frederick Scholte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Scholte) (Wikipedia; cites naturalisation record, probate calendar)
- Anderson & Sheppard's own history says Scholte, a Dutch cutter and master tailor, began working at 7 Savile Row around 1900 and developed the "London Cut or English Drape" there. This is a maker's claim. — [History, Anderson & Sheppard](https://www.anderson-sheppard.co.uk/about-us/our-history/)
- Per the same page he kept a low profile and was reluctant to take new customers, even within the Royal circle. — [History, Anderson & Sheppard](https://www.anderson-sheppard.co.uk/about-us/our-history/)
- Wikipedia says his shop overlooked Henry Poole & Co; it gives no number. (Poole is at 15 Savile Row; I did not open a page confirming Scholte's side of the street.) — [Frederick Scholte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Scholte)
- *Tailor & Cutter*, 1 Nov 1917, p. 16, "Searchlights on Style (IV.). The Scholte Lounge", praised his craftsmanship and called his house one "making history". Seen only as cited on Wikipedia; I did not open the original. — [Frederick Scholte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Scholte)
- Drape features as described by The Rake: small, high armhole, extra fullness in the sleeve head, natural shoulder, full chest with vertical drape. Colin Heywood (A&S managing director) explains the high armhole allows free movement. — [The Timeless Elegance of British Drape](https://therake.com/stories/british-drape), Ben St George, The Rake, April 2017
- Derek Guy: Scholte, "a Dutch tailor", is credited with the London cut or English drape; Per Anderson learned his trade from him. The cut has more room over chest and shoulder blades, soft folds from the collarbone, high armholes, unpadded shoulders. — [We Got It For Free: Anderson & Sheppard's A Style Is Born](https://www.putthison.com/we-got-it-for-free-anderson-sheppards-a-style/), Put This On, 27 Oct 2011
- Footprints of London calls him a "young radical Dutch tailor" who came to London around 1900 and trained Per Anderson, who later joined trouser cutter Sidney Sheppard (A&S, founded 1906 per A&S). — [The Great Tailors of London: Anderson and Sheppard](https://footprintsoflondon.com/2015/06/the-great-tailors-of-london-anderson-and-sheppard), 1 June 2015
- Principal tailor to the Prince of Wales (later Edward VIII, Duke of Windsor) from 1919 to his death in 1948, per Wikipedia. A&S says the Prince admired and praised the new style. — [Frederick Scholte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Scholte); [History, A&S](https://www.anderson-sheppard.co.uk/about-us/our-history/)
- The Met holds Scholte garments from the Duchess of Windsor (gift, 1974): a morning suit of 1936 (jacket by F. Scholte, trousers by Forster & Son; label "H.M. The King, 22.6.36") and an evening suit dated 1938-65 (Scholte and H. Harris, American). Page also notes the Prince popularised midnight blue evening wear. — [Morning suit](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/82171); [Evening suit](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/82170), The Met
- Historic Royal Palaces holds a green Lord of the Isles tartan suit worn by the Duke, jacket by Scholte, "favoured London tailor", worn as early as the 1960s; trousers made in New York. — [Lord of the Isles tartan suit](https://www.hrp.org.uk/blog/lord-of-the-isles-tartan-suit-worn-by-the-duke-of-windsor/), HRP, 11 Sep 2018
- Wikipedia says he apprenticed with the Household Cavalry (the Guards) and carried wide shoulders, roomy armholes and narrow waists from their uniforms into his cut. Only Wikipedia says this; no other source I opened mentions the Guards. — [Frederick Scholte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Scholte)
- Family: married Emma Lewellen 1888, four children; sons Lewellen (OBE 1919) and Owen (MC, flying ace, d. 1918). — [Frederick Scholte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Scholte)

## Disagreements and myths
- Dates of service: Wikipedia says 1919-1948 and calls 1959 a mistake. A Styleforum post (forum, unverified, cites a GQ UK interview) says 1919-1959. The HRP tartan suit "worn as early as the 1960s" with a Scholte jacket and the Met evening suit dated 1938-65 show garments outliving him or his firm; the firm may have continued after his death (my inference, no source). Do not state 1959 as fact.
- Who invented the drape: the usual attribution (Rake, Wikipedia, A&S) is to Scholte. Both The Rake and A&S describe it as made for/admired by the Prince; A&S says Per Anderson "developed the cut himself" after apprenticing. Wikipedia dates the drape to the 1930s, but A&S dates Scholte's work on it to c.1900 and Footprints says c.1900 arrival; the sources conflict on timing.
- Origin of the street address: A&S says 7 Savile Row c.1900; Wikipedia gives none. Single maker source.
- Guards link is Wikipedia only. The Rake mentions only a general military heritage of British tailoring.
- Styleforum story that film costume designers studied his suits at the V&A: forum hearsay, not verified.

## Construction notes (garments and details)
- Drape: high small armhole, fullness in sleeve head, natural unpadded shoulder, extra chest and blade room, soft vertical folds from collarbone. Richard Anderson reportedly called his shoulders the best on the Row (forum, unverified). Met morning suit: label inside "H.M. The King, 22.6.36", collars and cuffs repaired.
