# Tracht (style)

## Claims
- "Tracht" meant, until the 20th century, the clothing and adornment that marked a person's legal status, religion, rank or trade; only later did it come to mean supposedly timeless rustic dress. — [Trachten (AEIOU), Austria-Forum](https://austria-forum.org/af/AEIOU/Trachten/Trachten_english)
- Modern trachten blended regional dress with contemporary fashion; fashion influence grew once status-based dress codes were relaxed in the 18th and early 19th centuries. — [Trachten (AEIOU)](https://austria-forum.org/af/AEIOU/Trachten/Trachten_english)
- Interest in rural costume grew at the Bavarian and Austrian courts in the early 19th century, as part of a cultural revival after the Napoleonic invasions. Joseph von Hazzi gave the first extensive description of regional dress; F. J. von Lipowsky published a Bavarian survey in 1830. — [Tracht, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracht)
- 1835: a costume parade at Oktoberfest marked the silver wedding of Ludwig I and Therese. Maximilian II had traditional dress recognised as proper at court and wrote in 1849 that it mattered for national sentiment. — [Tracht, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracht)
- The first Trachtenverein is dated 1859 (Miesbach) by Wikipedia; the Lederhosen article instead dates a preservation society to 1883 (teacher Joseph Vogl, Leitzach Valley and Bayrischzell), which became the model spreading from Munich to Salzburg and Vienna. — [Tracht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracht); [Lederhosen, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lederhosen)
- 1895: Maximilian Schmidt organised an Oktoberfest parade of about 1,400 people in 150 groups. After this the Alpine kit (Lederhosen and dirndl) came to stand for "German" folk dress in general. — [Tracht, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracht)
- Urban bourgeoisie and aristocrats, inspired by rural life (Heimatkunst, Heimatwerk), drove preservation and revival in the 19th and early 20th centuries; most local costumes are fairly recent. Tourism and Sommerfrische (summer stays) were part of the shaping. — [Trachten (AEIOU)](https://austria-forum.org/af/AEIOU/Trachten/Trachten_english); [Erfundene Tradition, Jüdische Allgemeine](https://www.juedische-allgemeine.de/kultur/erfundene-tradition)
- Lederhosen probably go back to the late Middle Ages as hard-wearing work, riding and hunting breeches. The above-the-knee Alpine cut is first described in writing by August Lewald, touring the Tuxertal in Tyrol, in 1835. — [Lederhosen, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lederhosen)
- At the Joanneum folk-life museum, Aussee men's dress c. 1870 has Lederhosen worn over the knee, with a shorter, knee-baring version in fashion from about 1850. — [Display case 7, Museum Joanneum](https://www.museum-joanneum.at/en/folk-life-museum/our-programme/exhibitions/display-cases-trachtensaal/display-case-7)
- Archduke Johann (1782-1859) is shown in Styrian dress of grey loden in Leopold Kupelwieser's 1828 painting; the image entered Austrian collective memory. He took over Styrian estates from 1818 and was nicknamed the "Styrian Prince". — [The Styrian Prince, habsburger.net](https://www.habsburger.net/en/chapter/styrian-prince)
- The Steireranzug developed in Styria from the mid-19th century out of the hunters' dress of the Salzkammergut, Eisenwurzen and Mürz upland, borrowing from military uniform. Johann popularised it (a letter to Anna Plochl speaks of introducing "the grey coat"). Vienna distrusted it after the Alpenbund affair; in 1823 the Hofkanzlei banned state officials from wearing it. Franz Joseph later wore it hunting at Mürzsteg. — [Steireranzug, AustriaWiki (Austria-Forum)](https://austria-forum.org/af/AustriaWiki/Steireranzug)
- Spread of the Steireranzug: nobles first, as leisure wear, then the upper bourgeoisie; across Austria after WWI; Viktor von Geramb founded the Steirisches Heimatwerk in 1934; the Ständestaat treated it as dress of loyal citizens, the Nazis promoted it in Styria, and after 1945 it stayed popular in conservative circles. — [Steireranzug, AustriaWiki](https://austria-forum.org/af/AustriaWiki/Steireranzug)
- Joanneum: Geramb dated the loden "steirische Frackerl" (little tailcoat-shaped jacket) to c. 1840; the Mürz Valley hunter figure is c. 1830; the buttoned "Tschariwari" trousers were adapted from military trousers of the wars against Napoleon. — [Display case 7, Museum Joanneum](https://www.museum-joanneum.at/en/folk-life-museum/our-programme/exhibitions/display-cases-trachtensaal/display-case-7)
- The Janker (also Joppe, "Habsburger coat") is a boxy hip-length Alpine loden jacket, traditionally grey, green or brown, with horn buttons. A long version, the Innsbrucker, serves as a coat. Dress versions in finer loden often have a back box pleat ending in a half-belt. — [What to Look for in a Janker, High Latitude Style](https://highlatitudestyle.com/fashion-history-janker/) (a blog; no date of origin given)
- Loden: coarse, oily mountain-sheep wool, loosely woven, fulled in hot water, brushed and clipped, repeated until dense and windproof. — [High Latitude Style](https://highlatitudestyle.com/fashion-history-janker/)
- Lodenfrey (Munich) says it was founded in 1842 by weaver Johann Georg Frey; its water-repellent loden won a gold medal at Paris in 1855; Johann Baptist Frey made "Strichloden" in 1872, leading to the loden coat; the page says Franz Joseph I led the nobility wearing loden for hunting. Maker's own claims. — [Our history, Lodenfrey](https://www.lodenfrey.eu/en/unsere-geschichte)
- Wikipedia's Loden-Frey article: Maximilian II visited the first shop in 1852; a clothing factory for industrial loden coats was added in 1927/28; the firm profited from Aryanisation and used forced labour in the Nazi period. — [Loden-Frey, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loden-Frey)
- The Tracht business was commercial and partly Jewish-run: Moritz and Julius Wallach founded the Munich Volkskunsthaus in 1900, were synonymous with Bavarian Tracht by the 1920s, dressed the 1911 Oktoberfest, and were "Aryanised" in 1938. The 1930 operetta Im weißen Rössl popularised the dirndl. Assimilated Jews wore Tracht in Sommerfrische. — [Erfundene Tradition, Jüdische Allgemeine](https://www.juedische-allgemeine.de/kultur/erfundene-tradition)
- Nazi period: Gertrud Pesendorfer, Reich commissioner for Tracht work, designed a reduced "germanic" dress in the 1930s; the "Wiesn-Dirndl" took shape then. — [Erfundene Tradition](https://www.juedische-allgemeine.de/kultur/erfundene-tradition)
- A Tracht renaissance in the 1960s-70s, helped by the hippie interest in folklore, made it fashionable beyond Bavaria (a retailer's blog claim). — [Bavarian Tracht history, trachten.de](https://trachten.de/en/blog/bavarian-tracht-history)

## Disagreements and myths
- First Trachtenverein: 1859 Miesbach (Wikipedia, Tracht) vs 1883 Vogl, Leitzach Valley/Bayrischzell (Wikipedia, Lederhosen). They may be different associations; unresolved.
- "Ancient, timeless dress": the Austrian encyclopedia and the Jüdische Allgemeine both stress that modern Tracht is largely an invented or revived tradition from urban elites, and that many forms are recent.
- Which ruler promoted it: trachten.de credits Ludwig II; Wikipedia credits Maximilian II (court, 1849) and Ludwig II (clubs). Retailer blog is the weaker source.
- Origin of the Steireranzug: Austria-Forum says mid-19th century, yet the Joanneum dates Styrian loden jackets to c. 1830-40, and the 1823 ban and 1828 portrait are earlier. Dates for "the" suit vary with what counts as the suit.
- Lodenfrey's "first water-repellent loden, 1855" and "first functional material" are the firm's own claims.

## Construction notes (garments and details)
- Lederhosen: leather (deerskin per a retailer blog), black or brown traditionally; bib front (Latz) and braces are typical but not sourced in opened pages; decorative stitching in 3, 5, 7 or 9 designs along side seam and bib; side seams tied or buttoned at the hem; inseam vertical or a horizontal Tellernaht. Bavarian short; Swabian knee-length Bundhose with cuffs. — [Lederhosen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lederhosen)
- Steireranzug, grey with green trim, flap pockets and Lampassen (stripes on trousers): Altsteirer/Salonsteirer with green lapel, shoulder straps (Achselspangen), four buttons; Leobner/Jägerrock with stand collar, five buttons; green cloth waistcoat with silver buttons; loden or worsted; genuine buttons of stag horn. Hat: grey-green felt, dark green band, Gamsbart or capercaillie feather. — [Steireranzug](https://austria-forum.org/af/AustriaWiki/Steireranzug)
- Janker: boxy, hip-length, round or stand collar held by two oblong horn buttons, horn or eagle-stamped metal buttons, dress version with back box pleat and half-belt. — [High Latitude Style](https://highlatitudestyle.com/fashion-history-janker/)
- Charivari: silver chain worn with Lederhosen, may carry heirlooms or hunting trophies. — [trachten.de](https://trachten.de/en/blog/bavarian-tracht-history)
