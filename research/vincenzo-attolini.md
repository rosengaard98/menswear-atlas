# Vincenzo Attolini (person)

## Claims
- Life dates: none found. No opened source gives a birth or death year. QuiFinanza says only that he stayed master cutter until his death. — [Nel cuore di Napoli, dove nasce l'abito perfetto](https://quifinanza.it/lifestyle/nel-cuore-di-napoli-dove-nasce-labito-perfetto/669107/)
- Training: The Rake says he was an apprentice of Salvatore Morziello (single source; Morziello not otherwise checked). — [The History and Anatomy of Neapolitan Tailoring](https://therake.com/stories/the-history-and-anatomy-of-neapolitan-tailoring)
- He was head cutter at Gennaro Rubinacci's tailoring house, London House, in Naples. — [The Italian Tailor, Naples, and the Neapolitan Jacket](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/the-italian-tailor-naples-and-the-neapolitan-jacket); [The Birthplace of the Modern Neapolitan Suit](https://sprezzaboutique.com/blogs/editorial/the-birthplace-of-the-modern-neapolitan-suit)
- Credited with the trademark soft Neapolitan jacket made at London House: padding and lining removed, light enough to fold many times (Parisian Gentleman says eight; a journalist's flourish). — [Parisian Gentleman](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/the-italian-tailor-naples-and-the-neapolitan-jacket); [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/the-history-and-anatomy-of-neapolitan-tailoring)
- In the 1930s Gennaro "Bebè" Rubinacci proposed an even less structured version, which Attolini developed with ideas from the Roman tailor Domenico Caraceni (The Rake). The Parisian Gentleman article says Caraceni's influence is uncertain. — [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/the-history-and-anatomy-of-neapolitan-tailoring)
- Sprezza Boutique frames it as Gennaro and Attolini working together, with no single inventor named, and as a reworking of Savile Row rather than a rejection of it. — [Sprezza Boutique](https://sprezzaboutique.com/blogs/editorial/the-birthplace-of-the-modern-neapolitan-suit)
- Colleagues were at first sceptical of his light jacket, which went against fashion; QuiFinanza calls him "padre fondatore" of Neapolitan tailoring. — [QuiFinanza](https://quifinanza.it/lifestyle/nel-cuore-di-napoli-dove-nasce-labito-perfetto/669107/)
- Family firm's own account: Vincenzo was a young Neapolitan tailor; the firm dates from 1930, the year he designed and sewed a jacket with no padding, shoulder pads or lining, "as soft and lightweight as a shirt". Workshop on Via Vetriera, Naples. The page does not mention London House or Rubinacci. MAKER'S CLAIM. — [Roots and wings (Cesare Attolini, origins)](https://cesareattolini.com/pages/origins)
- Same page names clients (Toto, De Sica, Mastroianni, Clark Gable, Vittorio Emanuele III, the Duke of Windsor, who reportedly admired a jacket seen in Capri). Maker's own claim, not checked elsewhere. QuiFinanza independently lists De Sica, Mastroianni, Toto and the Duke of Windsor. — [Cesare Attolini origins](https://cesareattolini.com/pages/origins); [QuiFinanza](https://quifinanza.it/lifestyle/nel-cuore-di-napoli-dove-nasce-labito-perfetto/669107/)
- Son Cesare Attolini; Wikipedia says Cesare set up scale production and marketing, and grandsons Giuseppe and Massimiliano now run the brand. The Rake dates Cesare's "Neapolitan System" of specialist craftsmen to the 1960s. Family firm based in Casalnuovo di Napoli. — [Cesare Attolini (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesare_Attolini); [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/the-history-and-anatomy-of-neapolitan-tailoring)
- Massimiliano Attolini on his grandfather: he made the jacket "more like a second skin". Family recollection. — [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/the-history-and-anatomy-of-neapolitan-tailoring)
- Family says bespoke patterns are kept in cloth rather than paper, as Vincenzo and his team worked; a photo of his pattern hangs in reception; the author notes he was rather small. — [Permanent Style, Inside Cesare Attolini](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2026/08/english-cloth-english-bottoms-inside-cesare-attolini.html)
- A later namesake, grandson Vincenzo Attolini (eldest son of Cesare), founded Stile Latino; do not confuse with the subject. — [Parisian Gentleman, Stile Latino](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/stile-latino-by-vincenzo-attolini)
- Wikipedia (one source only): Vincenzo Attolini and Antonio Caggiula named as 1920s tailors who turned Naples from the heavy British model to unstructured, lighter jackets, citing Motta, Fashion through History (2018), and Gent Style (2025). — [Neapolitan tailoring (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_tailoring)

## Disagreements and myths
- Date of the soft jacket: Cesare Attolini says 1930; Wikipedia (Neapolitan tailoring) says 1920s with Caggiula; The Rake says 1930s following Rubinacci's suggestion. Rubinacci's London House opened 1931 (QuiFinanza) or 1932 (Wikipedia Rubinacci, Sprezza, The Rake on Gennaro), so a 1920s or 1930 date sits oddly with him being London House head cutter. Treat all as unresolved.
- Founder vs employee: the family presents him as founder of the Attolini firm; the other sources present him as Rubinacci's cutter. These can both be true in sequence, but no source says when he left London House, or if he did.
- Renaming of London House to Rubinacci: 1963 (Wikipedia Rubinacci) vs 1970 (QuiFinanza).
- "Folds up eight times" is journalistic colour.
- Permanent Style (2026) says it does not know the Attolini/Rubinacci relationship, so the link is not documented there.

## Construction notes (garments and details)
- Jacket: no shoulder pad or minimal; no or little canvas/lining; soft "shirt" shoulder; Cesare Attolini today describes its cut as Neapolitan on top, English below (Massimiliano, Permanent Style). Armhole and sleeve-head details are in Wikipedia's Cesare Attolini article (slim waist, high armholes, generous sleeve-head) and attach to the house style, not verified to Vincenzo himself.
