# Savile Row (context, region uk)

## Claims
- Savile Row was built between 1731 and 1735 on the Burlington Estate, on land called Ten Acres owned by the merchant tailor William Maddox; it was first called Savile Street. — [Savile Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row)
- The name comes from Lady Dorothy Savile, wife of the 3rd Earl of Burlington. — [Savile Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row); [London Museum](https://londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/savile-row-the-heart-of-london-tailoring)
- It began as a residential street for army officers and politicians; houses at first stood only on the east side, with the west side built up in the 19th century. — [Savile Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row)
- Tailors reached Cork Street about 1790 and Savile Row itself by 1803 (Wikipedia); The Rake says the first firm set up by 1805. — [Savile Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row); [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/savile-row-and-the-future-of-tailoring)
- Henry Poole opened a Savile Row entrance in 1846 from premises in Old Burlington Street; the London Museum calls Poole thought to be the first tailor on the Row itself, and Wikipedia's tailoring article calls Poole "Founder of Savile Row". — [London Museum](https://londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/savile-row-the-heart-of-london-tailoring); [Savile Row tailoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row_tailoring)
- Poole is credited with the dinner jacket, made as a smoking jacket for the Prince of Wales (later Edward VII); Wikipedia's Savile Row article dates it 1860. This is a house tradition, not independently checked here. — [Savile Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row); [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/savile-row-and-the-future-of-tailoring)
- Other houses: H. Huntsman & Sons founded 1849, moved to No. 11 in 1919; Dege & Skinner at No. 10 from 1865; Anderson & Sheppard founded 1906 (The Rake: No. 13); Hawkes & Co at No. 1 from 1912, later Gieves & Hawkes (merged 1974 per Wikipedia); T & F French and A.H. Kilgour merger 1923. — [London Museum](https://londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/savile-row-the-heart-of-london-tailoring); [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/savile-row-and-the-future-of-tailoring); [Savile Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row)
- Gieves & Hawkes is credited as the first Savile Row house to offer ready-to-wear. — [Savile Row tailoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row_tailoring); [Savile Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row)
- Tailoring was a low-paid trade; a strike in 1867 involved about 2,500 tailors, the Association of London Master Tailors formed in 1891, and in 1903 Cooling, Lawrence & Sons ended floor-based "sittings". — [Savile Row tailoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row_tailoring)
- Early 1900s: Frederick Scholte's English drape (natural shoulders, high armhole, full chest roll) was popularised with Anderson & Sheppard; Per Anderson, a Scholte protege, developed the "London cut" as the A&S house style. — [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/savile-row-and-the-future-of-tailoring); [Savile Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row)
- Hardy Amies opened his house at No. 14 in 1946 (Wikipedia) or 1949 (London Museum); his couture was for women, and he later moved into menswear. — [Savile Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row); [London Museum](https://londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/savile-row-the-heart-of-london-tailoring)
- Apple Corps took No. 3 in 1968; the rooftop concert was on 30 January 1969. — [Savile Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row); [London Museum](https://londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/savile-row-the-heart-of-london-tailoring)
- Nutters of Savile Row opened at No. 35a on 14 February 1969 (Wikipedia), founded by Tommy Nutter and Edward Sexton, backed by Cilla Black and Peter Brown; The Rake calls it the first new firm on the Row in generations and the first with a window display. Clients included Mick Jagger and Twiggy; three of the four Beatles wore Nutters on the Abbey Road cover (London Museum). Nutter left in 1976 for Kilgour and died in 1992. — [Savile Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row); [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/savile-row-and-the-future-of-tailoring); [London Museum](https://londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/savile-row-the-heart-of-london-tailoring)
- Chittleborough & Morgan (No. 12) was founded in 1981 by former Nutters cutters. Richard James opened in 1992. Ozwald Boateng set up at 9 Vigo Street in 1995. The 1990s "New Bespoke Movement" (Boateng, Timothy Everest, Richard James) brought bolder colour and younger clients. — [Savile Row tailoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row_tailoring); [London Museum](https://londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/savile-row-the-heart-of-london-tailoring); [Savile Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row)
- The Savile Row Bespoke Association was founded in 2004; members must put at least 50 hours of hand labour into a two-piece suit. The street became a Special Policy Area in 2016; Kathryn Sargent was the first woman to open a house on the Row that year. — [Savile Row tailoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row_tailoring); [Savile Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savile_Row)
- Today many houses sell ready-to-wear and made-to-measure alongside bespoke; The Rake quotes trade figures expecting rents to favour larger houses. — [The Rake](https://therake.com/stories/savile-row-and-the-future-of-tailoring)

## Disagreements and myths
- Poole's number: No. 37 and No. 32 both appear in Wikipedia; London Museum says No. 32 and now No. 15; The Rake says No. 37. Unclear; avoid a number.
- Huntsman: No. 11 from 1919 (London Museum, Wikipedia) vs No. 15 (The Rake).
- Hardy Amies: 1946 vs 1949.
- First tailor on the Row: 1803 (Wikipedia), 1805 (The Rake), Poole 1846 "first on the Row itself" (London Museum). Likely different definitions of first.
- Dinner jacket for Edward VII is a Poole house claim; no independent check opened.
- "Bespoke" as "spoken for" cloth is a trade etymology (Wikipedia tailoring article); the Savile Row article says tailors claim 17th-century use and notes a 2008 Advertising Standards Authority ruling allowing it for machine-sewn made-to-measure.
- The Rake's profit-margin claim is the author's assertion; do not use.
- Wikipedia says William Maddox bought land 1622 (tailoring article); the main article says the street was built 1731-35 on his land.

## Construction notes (garments and details)
- Not a garment entry. Trade definition: bespoke is cut and made by hand to one customer's measurements with several fittings (London Museum). The Rake: traditional suits had a single inside pocket and unlined trousers; modern clients expect uniform machine-like stitching.
